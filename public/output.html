Log

Studio's scene: add sources, order the layers, and show or hide them here or from the Stream Deck. The Mac app sends the scene to extra displays, a Blackmagic UltraStudio or DeckLink, or NDI® (below) for your switcher.

Video output

4K: every NDI and Blackmagic output sends 2160p at its own frame rate (2160p50 for 1080p50), with the same graphics, scenes and links as in HD; press HD to go back. A Blackmagic device's own 4K formats can also be picked for its output; in HD it sends the 1080p one at that rate. Use it only with 4K gear: an HD switcher can't take 2160p. Text, shapes and logos drawn by Studio are sharp; pictures and videos are only as sharp as their files, so a 1080p video or a small logo looks softer.

Sending the scene to a display, a Blackmagic device or NDI needs the FirstPass Studio Mac app (this copy of Studio was started another way).

Tick it when everything on this output is seen on the stream, like a second downstream key: then its sponsors and Sponsor ads count in the exposure report. Leave it off for a camera's upstream key or a commentary monitor, as Studio can't tell when they're on air and counting them would overclaim; its Sponsor ads then don't take a sponsor's turn. Output 1 always counts. An output counts as on air only while it's on: turned off or set to Web links only, it isn't.

Sources are switched to chroma key safe, which limits some of their options: panels are solid, soft shadows, glows and light rays are left out, nothing is half see-through (fades become cuts, soft edges are hard), and options that only fade, like the countdown's fade and the ad's glow, are skipped. Titles, logos, light and dark modes and every other setting work as usual. Nothing is saved over: pick another output, or untick this, and every source is back exactly as it was.

The sound goes into the SDI as 48 kHz stereo, in time with the picture. On the switcher, turn on the sound of the input the fill comes into (On, or AFV to follow the picture). An SDI to HDMI converter passes it on.

Switcher key settings: leave Straight off and turn the switcher's Pre Multiplied Key (shaped) on; clip and gain are then not used, and soft edges and shadows key cleanly. With Pre Multiplied Key off, clip and gain cut into the soft edges and the fill's edges go dark. Tick Straight only if the switcher can't do a premultiplied key (every ATEM can).

Straight is ticked: into a Pre Multiplied Key it leaves a light fringe round logos and text. Untick it and turn the switcher's Pre Multiplied Key on.

Goes with

This output shows its own graphics: turn sources on and off for it with its coloured buttons in the Sources list (they start off), or its own Stream Deck links. It plays the sound of what it shows, the music if Music plays on ticks it, and the mics that go to it. Scenes and sequences are shared by every output: pick its tab over the Scene View and a click plays them on this output.

Turn the output off to change these settings.

The scene fills each display full screen, scaled to fit. Fill is the scene over black; key is white wherever the scene shows and black where it's see-through (grey where it's partly see-through). Sound plays from the fill or chroma display's window through the Mac's audio output. Each output opens full screen in its own window: leave full screen (the green button or ⌃⌘F) to move it to another display or get at other apps. To turn it off from the display itself, click it and press Esc, or close its window. Blackmagic: needs Blackmagic Desktop Video installed, and nothing else (such as Media Express) using the device; the scene plays at the video format's frame rate, its sound in the SDI (on the fill) unless Sound says otherwise. Pick the format the switcher runs at. NDI: needs NDI's runtime for Mac (free, ndi.link/NDIRedistV6Apple) installed on this Mac. The sources show up on the network as this Mac's name (Source name Fill) and (Source name Key), or (Source name Chroma), or just (Source name) with transparency. The source name is the output's name, and changes with it when the output is renamed, unless Use my own name is ticked; a source that changes name has to be picked again on the receiver. pick them on the switcher or in its NDI converter. Each sends at the video format's frame rate only while something is receiving it. Use a wired network: over Wi-Fi frames get lost and moving graphics such as the ticker stutter. The sound plays through the Mac's audio output, not over NDI. Stream Deck: /deck/output/toggle (also on and off) shows or blanks the output without changing this setup.

Stinger

    Expand for info

    Studio controls a Blackmagic ATEM switcher over the network: the Mac and the switcher need to be on the same network, ideally a wired one. Look for switchers finds them the way ATEM Software Control does (the first time, macOS asks to let Studio use the local network: say Allow), and Studio remembers the one you pick by its name, so it finds it again if its address changes. Click an input to put it on preview, then Cut or Auto to take it, or Stinger: Studio plays the scene's Stinger page on the output on a downstream key (Output 1 unless another one that's on is; the card says if that output is off, as it is when set to Web links only) and cuts in the middle of it, where the bands cover the frame (read from the stinger's own settings, so a longer hold still lines up). If that output's picture reaches the switcher a little late, set its line-up in frames (each output keeps its own) and check it with Test the line-up before you're live. Studio shows only what your switcher has (an ATEM Mini Pro has 4 inputs, one upstream key and one downstream key; a Mini Extreme has 8, four and two). The switcher's own buttons, ATEM Software Control and other controllers keep working alongside Studio, and Studio shows what they do. Each output's Into the switcher as (under Video output) checks the key it comes into, and Set up this key for me sets that key's inputs, type and Pre Multiplied Key when you press it. An output on an upstream key can tick the cameras it goes with: Studio's cuts take its key on and off with them on the same frame, and with Keys follow the camera a cut on the switcher's panel brings it a frame or two later. It sends nothing to the switcher by itself, not even when it connects or reconnects, and if the switcher stops answering nothing is saved up for later. Stream Deck: /deck/switcher/cut, auto, stinger (or stinger/2), ftb, preview/2 or preview/Camera%202, program/2, usk/1/toggle (on, off), dsk/1/toggle (on, off, auto) and macro/1. The Stream Deck plugin's Switcher key presses these and lights like the switcher's buttons: red on program, green on preview.

    Steps

    Expand for info

    Each step turns its sources on together and keeps them on for its length; when it ends, sources the next step doesn't use go off with their own fades. Until it finishes waits for the step's videos to end (and pages that switch themselves off when done), and moves on after the video's length plus 3 s, or a minute for a page, if the end never comes. Until the game moves on holds until the clock starts, or the next break or full time. A sequence that starts by itself waits its few seconds after the siren first. Repeat until finishes the step on screen when its moment comes (or the next sequence's start comes), then ends. A sequence only turns off sources it turned on itself; to keep sources on for the whole sequence, start it with a scene that turns them on. + Add scene goes to a scene, as a starting point or a reset point: what the scene turns on stays on under the next steps, and after the sequence. It goes straight on, or holds like any step. + Add sponsor ads plays the next ads from the Sponsors page, taking turns fairly by tier: a video until it ends, a still for its seconds, in the Sponsor ad sources it adds to the scene (the House ad when no sponsor has an ad, unless unticked). + Add sequence plays another sequence's steps there (but not its repeat or Then; its music plays while it does, when this sequence has none of its own), so a mini sequence can be built once and used in many; a sequence can't go inside itself.

    Sound plays in the Scene View only while Video output is off, for testing. With a video output on, the output carries the sound and the Scene View is silent.

    The Scene View shows the scene as it is right now, whether or not your switcher has it on air. It runs every page in the scene a second time, so turn Live View off on stream nights if the Mac is busy. The dashed box is the selected source. Pop out shows the Scene View in its own window that you can resize, move to another screen or make full screen, with On/Off buttons for the sources floating in it; the one here turns off while it's open, and closing it brings it back. The Scene View's sound needs Live View on, and only one Output page plays it at a time: click a Scene View to bring the sound to it. The line above says why it's silent, if it is.

      Stream Deck links

      Expand for info

      The FirstPass Studio plugin's Source key is the easiest way: pick this source in its settings and it lights up while the source is on, and keeps working if you rename it. These links work in a Stream Deck Website action (tick GET request in background) or the Timed Sequence plugin. They carry this Mac's Stream Deck key after the port, so a web page can't press them; a link without it is refused, so copy older links again from here. Renaming the source changes its links.

      Media folder

        Expand for info

        Every song, video and image added here, under Music or on the Sponsors page. Remove deletes the file from the media folder for good (with a levelled song's original, kept in originals), and asks first, listing where it's used. Something on air (a source showing it, or the song playing) can't be removed until it's off. Playlists, the super shot song and sponsors' ads let go of it; sources, page themes and sequence music keep its name, shown as (missing), so their settings and Stream Deck keys stay until you pick another file. A .mov and the WebM converted from it are separate files: remove each one you don't want.

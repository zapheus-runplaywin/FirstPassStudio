Competition setup

Every graphic is set up for one competition at a time.

Open competition setupOpen athlete photos

The Athletes page keeps a photo of each athlete for the position match ups. Pick a team to see every athlete in its PlayHQ line ups this season and add a photo to each (or drop one onto them). Teams are listed by division, so a club with a team in more than one division (Knights) has a button in each, showing that division's line ups. Photos are saved in the competition's photos folder, named after the athlete, so they match in every game. To add many at once, drop photos named after their athletes (Jane Smith.jpg or smith_jane.png); any that match nobody can be given to an athlete in the photo library. An athlete who plays in more than one division needs only one photo (All athletes lists everyone once). Without a photo, graphics show a generic silhouette for the athlete's gender: picked on the page if you've picked one, else PlayHQ's gender for the athlete, else the PlayHQ gender of their division when they play in a men's or women's division (mixed and open divisions leave it neutral). Silhouette on each athlete shows where it came from and can override it. Games on an athlete records their games played per division (optionally per club) as of a date, and the positions they've played; PlayHQ games after that date are added on automatically.

The competition setup page keeps a saved setup for each competition (Thunder League, AMMNA Nationals, GIG WANL, or any you add) and switches between them. AMMNA Nationals and GIG WANL come with their PlayHQ link, colours and logo. AMMNA's competition is set to Australian Championships and GIG WANL's to GIG WANL, so both are ready to use. When an update improves one of these ready-made setups, yours picks up only what you haven't changed yourself. Each one has:

  • PlayHQ: the organisation's playhq.com page and which of its competitions and seasons to use. Find its competitions lists what PlayHQ has for that organisation, so you can pick the right name.
  • Names on the graphics: the competition, organisation and venue names, each blank to use PlayHQ's.
  • Game rules: quarters or halves and their length, and super shots, for the whole competition or per division. Under the super shots tick box each PlayHQ division can follow the competition, or have super shots or not. A setting can also be saved for a word instead of a whole division name, and then covers every division with that word in its name ("Open" covers "Division 1 Open"; "Men" covers "Men's" but not "Women's"); a division's own setting wins over a word. GIG WANL comes set for super shots in Open only, not 21&Under or Men's, by the words "Open", "21" and "Men". A division without super shots doesn't play the super shot animation for a +2 press or count goals as super shots, and its shooting stats and top shooters leave the Super column off.
  • Finals: the competition's finals format, and a different one for any division that needs it. Top 2: grand final, or best of three. Top 4: semis then grand final, Page (double chance for 1 and 2), or a placings day (1 v 2 and 3 v 4). Top 6: byes for 1 and 2, or a double chance for 1 and 2. Top 8: AFL style, straight knockout, or carnival placings (a knockout over three weeks with play-offs, so every team gets a place 1 to 8). Every team: everyone plays on grand final day, 1 v 2 for the title, 3 v 4 for third, 5 v 6 for fifth and so on down the ladder, with nobody knocked out (an odd team out has no game). The ladder marks the finals places by what each earns and the finals graphic follows it (see Finals below).
  • Game day: the court the scoreboard follows, whether it picks the game automatically, Electronic scoring starts [x] minutes before the game (default 10; set 20 or 30 when games are further apart), and how many days ahead counts as "this round". Automatic selection switches to the next game when its electronic scoring starts, and the scoreboard doesn't ask PlayHQ for a game's score before then, even for a game picked by hand: the control page says when it will start checking. That keeps PlayHQ's "game could not be found or was not electronically scored" error out of the debug log.
  • Colours and font: nine colours, how see-through the dark panels are, and the font, with a preview. Click a colour's swatch for a colour picker (shade square, hue slider, HEX and RGB boxes, and on Chrome or Edge Pick from screen), or type or paste a colour into its box as HEX (#7f4092) or RGB (127, 64, 146). Under Brand kit colours, paste any text from a brand kit with HEX or RGB values in it; those colours are saved with the competition and show in every picker, one click to use.
  • Logos: the competition logo, in a light mode version (for a light picture behind it) and a dark mode version (for a dark one; if you add only one, it is used for both), and the team logos. The logos page and title use the light mode logo unless ?mode=dark is added; the ladder's corner, on its coloured header, uses the dark mode one unless ?mode=light is added. Drop team logo files on the page; they match clubs by file name, and the PlayHQ team list shows which logo each team gets, with a box to pick a different one.
  • Clubs: a team's logo and colour belong to its club, so every team of a club in every division shares them, and changing one row changes them all. The club is the team's name without the division on the end ("Comets 21U", "Comets Men" and "Comets Open" are all Comets; "Stormz Div 2" is Stormz). The Club box in the team list links a team to a different club, or to a new one.
  • Club colours: in the same PlayHQ team list, Add colour gives a club its own colour. The scoreboard puts it behind its teams' names and The Clash behind their logo and name, with dark or white text, whichever reads better. Clubs without one (or after ×) use the competition's colours. The match up page always uses the competition's colours. Pick the colour with the colour picker or paste it as HEX or RGB.
  • Graphics: the emoji either side of the title banner (pick any emoji, or leave it empty for none) and The Clash's big word. Also the PlayHQ Client ID, which most competitions don't need.

Use this one switches every graphic, the control page and the Stream Deck to that competition: the round goes back to automatic and the scoreboard starts on that competition's first game. Saving the competition in use reloads the graphics with the changes. Setups are kept in the competitions folder (see Saved settings and git below).

Carnivals (like the AMMNA nationals) play several rounds a day: on those days "this round" moves on to each round as its first game comes up, and Up next looks across the whole day.

Output (the Studio scene)

Open the Output pagePreview

The Output page builds Studio's own scene: sources drawn as layers on one 1920 × 1080 frame, top layer first, and sends it to a display or a Blackmagic device for your switcher.

  • Sources: Add page adds any Studio graphic or web address; Add image adds a PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP or SVG, and Add video an MP4, WebM or MOV (saved in the data folder's media folder). Each opens a box to pick it. A new source goes above the selected one (on top if none is selected), hidden, and is selected so you can set it up straight away. Pages not in the scene yet are listed first, and Add all unused pages adds every one of them, hidden. Drag a source up or down the list to move it between layers, or use ↑ and ↓.
  • Page options: a Studio graphic's link options described on this page (position, heading, division, round, animate away and so on) show as fields under its Address, and are saved in the address.
  • On / Off fades a source in or out with its own Fade time in milliseconds (0 is a cut).
  • Position and scale place it on the frame; Full frame fits it to 1920 × 1080. Page size is the size a page is drawn at (1920 × 1080 for full-frame graphics).
  • Load the page fresh each time it's shown (on by default) reloads a page as it fades in so its animation replays, and unloads it when hidden. Untick it to keep a page running so it shows instantly. Refresh page reloads it now.
  • Videos start from the beginning each time they're shown (untick to carry on), can loop, and fade out when they finish unless looping. Sound turns a video's sound on or off, with a volume; it plays through the Mac's audio output. Restart video starts it again.
  • ProRes: the pages can't play ProRes, so a .mov added to the media folder is converted to a WebM beside it (keeping a ProRes 4444 clip's transparency; the Output page warns when a .mov has none to keep, such as H.264), and sources using it switch over when it's done. The original is kept. The Mac app includes ffmpeg to do this (running from a copy of the code instead, run vendor/ffmpeg/build.sh once or brew install ffmpeg). MP4 and WebM play as they are.
  • Stream Deck plugin: the Mac app includes a FirstPass Studio plugin for the Stream Deck app. Install it from the menu bar icon (Install the Stream Deck plugin) or the Output page's button. Its Source key shows or hides the source you pick in its settings (or fades it in, fades it out, restarts it, or turns a video's sound on or off) and lights up orange while the source is on, whether it was turned on from the key, the Output page or a link. It shows the source's name, keeps working when you rename it, and says off air while Studio isn't running.
  • Stream Deck links: /deck/layer/<name>/on, off, toggle and restart for each source, /deck/layer/<name>/sound/on, off and toggle for a video, named in lower case with dashes for spaces (Up Next is up-next), and /deck/layers/off to hide everything. The Output page copies each source's links.
  • Preview: the Output page shows the scene live; it runs the scene's pages a second time, so turn it off when the Mac is busy. Preview sound plays the sound from the preview, with or without an output on; a display or Blackmagic output is muted while it does, so it never plays twice; it goes back to the outputs when the Output page closes. Pop out shows the preview in its own window to resize, move to another screen or make full screen while testing (with the same sound), with a floating Sources list to show or hide each source from there; the Output page's own preview turns off while it's open and comes back when it closes.
  • Display output (Mac app): sends the scene full screen to the Mac's extra displays (HDMI into a switcher). Chroma puts it over a solid colour on one display (green, blue or any colour) for a switcher that keys on that colour. Fill and key uses two extra displays: fill is the scene over black, key is white where the scene shows and black where it's see-through, for the switcher's fill and key inputs. It's only offered with two extra displays connected (a plain M1 drives one; Pro and Max chips drive more). Each output opens full screen in its own window; leave full screen (green button or ⌃⌘F) to move it to another display or use other apps. While the output is on, its settings are locked: turn it off to change them. Output on/off, Esc on an output window, closing it, or /deck/output/on, off and toggle show or blank the displays without changing the setup.
  • Blackmagic output (Mac app): sends the scene out of a Blackmagic UltraStudio or DeckLink: fill and key with the device's external keyer (fill on one SDI out, key on the other) or fill only, in the video format you pick (pick the one the switcher runs at). The fill is premultiplied; tick Straight if the switcher's key wants it unpremultiplied. It needs Blackmagic Desktop Video installed and the device free, so quit any other app using it first. Output on/off and the /deck/output links work the same.

The scene only changes when you change it, so it adds no PlayHQ requests beyond those of the pages in it.

Control page settings (every page follows these)

Every graphic uses the round, game and court set on the scoreboard control page. A setting added to a page's own link (like ?division=2 or ?round=6) overrides it for that page only.

  • Round: Automatic picks the round played in the next 5 days, counting today, so a Tuesday round is picked from the Friday before (the number of days is on the competition setup page). Otherwise it's the last round played. On a day with several rounds, it moves on to each round as its first game comes up. Pick a round from the list to test with it, then set it back to Automatic.
  • Always a game on: when Studio starts, or the scoreboard is cleared, it puts a game on straight away with Follow PlayHQ on: the game for now on the court, otherwise this round's first game on that court, otherwise this round's first game. The board is only left empty if PlayHQ can't be reached or the competition isn't found (check the PlayHQ link and competition on the competition setup page).
  • Game: tick Pick the game automatically for court and set the court (it starts on the court set on the competition setup page, e.g. Indoor 01 or just 1). It shows the first game of the day on that court, then switches to each next game when its electronic scoring starts (10 minutes before by default, set on the setup page), with the quarter back to Q1 and a full clock. A final (a round with "Final" in its name) stays on until PlayHQ marks it finished, in case of extra time. With a round picked by hand, its games are treated as if they were today, so the switching can be tested any time.
  • Overrides: picking a game from the list turns automatic selection off until you tick it again or change the court. Pressing a score button switches scores to manual, and any clock button switches the clock to manual.
  • Follow PlayHQ: the score and quarter come from PlayHQ's live scoring every 5 seconds, and Follow the PlayHQ scorer's clock runs the clock from the scorer's clock too. Switching scores between Follow PlayHQ and Manual (on the control page, the Stream Deck, by pressing a score button or by picking a game) turns the clock option on or off to match, and the control page says so. Changing the clock after that still wins: untick the box or press a clock button to run the clock by hand while scores follow PlayHQ.
  • Pages that use the picked game's division: ladder and finals (without ?division=), ladder bar, top shooters, title, match up, The Clash, winner celebration, team lists, shooting stats and full time. The fixture, results, ticker and up next use the round.
  • Stream Deck: /deck/auto/on, /deck/auto/off, /deck/auto/toggle, /deck/court/2, /deck/clock/playhq/on and /deck/ticker/faster / slower. The Stream Deck page has the rest, with Copy buttons and the plugin. A score key switches scoring to Manual (Follow PlayHQ stops for the score and the clock), and a clock key stops the clock following the PlayHQ scorer.

Game graphics (for the game picked on the scoreboard)

The cards (match up, team lists, fixtures, ladders and the rest) sit in the space between the title at the top and the scoreboard at the bottom, with room around them. A card with more in it than fits (a long team list, a big ladder) is shrunk to fit. The animations (The Clash, winner, super shot and the logo animations) play in that space too. The ladder lower third sits right above the scoreboard, the same width as it.

Finals (one per division)

Who plays who in the division's finals, week by week, in the format set for that division on the competition setup page, with the teams from the ladder. Until the home and away games are finished it says If the season ended today. Each game shows the teams' ladder positions, logos and scores; the winner lights up and moves on to the next game, and the champions show under it once the grand final is decided. PlayHQ's finals games (rounds with "final" or "play-off" in their name) are used when it has them, with their day, time, court and score. PlayHQ often has no finals fixtures, so pick each winner (and the score, if you like) in Finals on the control page; a PlayHQ result always wins over one picked there. The ladder marks the same finals places: double chance or a week off in the highlight colour, elimination finals in the accent colour, playing for third in the small print colour, with a line under the last finals place and a key under the ladder (?key=0 leaves it off). Loads once. Options: ?week=2 shows one week only, ?title=Grand%20Final%20day changes the heading, ?seeds=0 leaves off the ladder positions, ?sub=Double%20chance%20for%20the%20top%202 changes the line under the heading (it says Top 4 finals and so on; ?sub=0 leaves it off), ?projected=0 leaves off "If the season ended today", ?logo=0 leaves off the competition logo (?mode=light uses the light mode one), and without ?division= it shows the division of the game picked on the control page.

Mac app

FirstPass Studio is a Mac app for Apple silicon Macs (M1 and later). Open it from Applications or Spotlight: the home page opens in the app's own window, and the 1P icon sits in the menu bar.

Studio only runs while a Studio window is open. Opening a window (opening the app, Open Studio or any page in the 1P menu, ⌘N) puts Studio on air: it serves the graphics and the Stream Deck links, talks to PlayHQ, and restarts by itself if it stops. Closing the last Studio window takes it off air, so nothing talks to PlayHQ and the graphics and Stream Deck get no answer until a window opens again. Closing the last window while on air asks first (Take Off Air or Cancel), and so does ⌘Q, so a stray keypress can't stop a stream. Keep a window open, such as the Output page, for the whole stream.

The 1P menu opens Studio, the control and setup pages, the settings folder and the debug log, and has Start Studio (opens the home page), Stop Studio (closes the Studio windows and goes off air), Restart Studio, Open at login and Check for updates. The line under the version says whether Studio is on air or off air. Opened at login, the app sits in the menu bar off air, unless you tick Open Studio at login, which opens the home page and goes on air. Updates download by themselves and install when the app quits, never mid-stream.

The app window is like any Mac app's: no address bar, tabs or bookmarks, the Studio header is the title bar (drag it to move the window), and it shows in the Dock and ⌘Tab while a window is open. Links that open a new tab on Studio (a preview, the control or setup page) open another window; links elsewhere (PlayHQ, GitHub) open in your browser. The menu at the top of the screen has File > Open in Browser (⇧⌘O) and Copy Page Link (⇧⌘C) for the page in front, Go for each Studio page (⌘1 to ⌘6), View for reload, back and zoom, New Window (⌘N), and Close Studio Windows (⌥⌘W) in the FirstPass Studio menu.

To use your browser instead, tick Open pages in the browser in the 1P menu. The app can't tell when browser tabs close, so then Studio stays on air from Start Studio until Stop Studio or Quit.

Building and releasing it is in the README under Desktop app (Mac).

Links (this Mac's .local name)

The links on this page, the control page and the Mac app's menu (Copy the Studio link too) start with this Mac's Bonjour name, , instead of localhost. It is the Local hostname in System Settings > General > Sharing, so the same link works on this Mac and from a phone, another computer or a Stream Deck on the same network. The port stays 8080, and http://localhost:8080 still works on this Mac, so links and Stream Deck buttons you already have carry on. To change it, set "linkHost" in config.json in the data folder: "bonjour" (the default), "localhost", or any name or IP address, then choose Restart Studio in the 1P menu.

Anything on the same network can open these pages and press the Stream Deck links (scores, clock, game), as it always could. Stream on a network you trust, not open venue Wi-Fi, or block incoming connections to FirstPass Studio in the Mac's firewall.

Saved settings

Everything Studio saves lives in one data folder on this computer, outside the app, so updates never touch it: ~/Library/Application Support/FirstPass Studio (the setup page shows where, and Open the settings folder in the 1P menu opens it).

  • competitions/: a folder per competition with its setup (settings.json), team logos, competition logo, athlete photos and athlete register, and active.json, the one in use.
  • control.json: the control page's choices (the round picked by hand, automatic game selection and its court, following the PlayHQ clock, how often to check PlayHQ, showing the clock, the ticker speed, finals winners picked by hand).
  • scoreboard-state.json: the game on the board, the scores and the clock.
  • config.json (optional): changes this computer's settings (port, log size, how often to check PlayHQ) from the app's defaults.
  • app-settings.json: the Mac app's own choices (whether Studio opens at login, opening pages in the browser instead of the app window, and where the window was).
  • logs/: the debug log. backups/: a copy of the folder made before an update changes how settings are saved.

Studio starts with no competitions. On the competition setup page, Import copies one of the import configs (ready-made competitions with their PlayHQ link, rules, colours and logos, kept in imports/) to use or change. Running from a copy of the repository, Save as import config writes a competition into imports/ to commit, so the next customer can import it. Export as template saves a competition with its logos as one .json file, and Import a template file adds it on any Studio. Delete this competition works on any competition; deleting the one in use switches the graphics to another.

When an update changes how settings are saved, Studio backs up the data folder and brings it up to date on its first start. Settings saved by a newer Studio open read-only in an older one.

Data and bandwidth

Only scoreboard.html, goals.html, ticker.html and the automatic supershot.html keep asking for new data (the control page does too, but it isn't a graphic). countdown.html also asks Studio which game is picked every 5 seconds, and ticker.html asks for its speed every 3 seconds, but neither reaches PlayHQ. The super shot page asks Studio every second for the scoreboard and nothing else, so it adds no PlayHQ requests. Every other graphic gets its data once when it loads, and again each time it's shown on the Output page (with Load the page fresh each time it's shown, the default). The Clash and the winner celebration only retry if Studio isn't running.

Pages only ever talk to Studio on this computer, which uses no internet. Studio is the only thing that contacts PlayHQ, and it caches what it gets:

  • Scoreboard following PlayHQ: one small request every 5 seconds (Check PlayHQ every on the control page), starting when the game's electronic scoring starts (set on the setup page). Nothing is asked before then.
  • Studio scene (scene.html and the Output page): nothing. They keep one connection open to the helper, which only sends when the scene changes; the pages in the scene ask for what they normally do.
  • Display output (Mac app): nothing. The output windows show the scene page, so they ask for what the scene's pages normally ask for, once per display (fill and key means the pages run twice). The Blackmagic output runs them once more, off screen.
  • Stream Deck plugin's Source keys: nothing. The plugin keeps one connection open to the helper while any of its keys is on screen, and the helper only sends when the scene changes.
  • Countdown: nothing. The clock ticks in the page and the start time comes from the game already picked.
  • Last 10 goals: nothing extra. It shares the scoreboard's 5 second reading, and Studio works out the goal order from it.
  • Ticker: other courts' scores at most every 30 seconds.
  • Automatic game selection: the fixture about once a minute for the current round, every 10 minutes for the rest.
  • Athletes page: when you open a team, the line up of each of its games this season, once (finished games are kept for an hour).
  • Finals: the division's ladder and its season's rounds when the page loads (the rounds are kept for 10 minutes, the current one for a minute).
  • Team lists: when the game's line up is incomplete, the same season line ups once to make up the squad (kept for 10 minutes).

Each answer is a few kilobytes, well under 1 MB an hour in total. Two options make a page poll, both off unless you add them: ?refresh= on the ladder, and &reload= on the scoreboard, scene and control page.

Something not working? The debug log is in the logs folder of the data folder, or open the debug log. Send logs/playhq-graphics.log to Claude.

On the Output page, Add page puts any of these graphics in the scene; turn each on or off there or from the Stream Deck. Animations run at the same speed at any frame rate, 50p or 60p.

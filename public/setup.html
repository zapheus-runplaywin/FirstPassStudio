Game rules

Halves give Scoreboard control H1 and H2 buttons and the shooting stats say "Half time" and "Full time". Without super shots the Super columns are left off the shooting stats and top shooters (Scoreboard control keeps its +2 and −2 buttons). Every division follows the tick box unless it has its own setting below. A setting saved for a word rather than a whole division name (GIG WANL comes with "Open", "21" and "Men") covers each division with that word in its name: "Open" covers "Division 1 Open", "Men" covers "Men's" but not "Women's". A division's own setting wins over a word. When the quarter clock gets to the last minutes (3 unless you change it), the scoreboard shows a small "Super shot" tab above its clock for the divisions with super shots, until the quarter ends; 0 leaves the tab off. The break lengths help when building a break sequence on the Output page: its editor says how many times one loop fits in the break. With halves, only half time is used.

Finals

Every division uses the competition's format unless it has its own. The ladder marks the finals places by what each one earns (double chance or week off, elimination, playing for third) with a line under the last one, and the finals graphic shows who plays who week by week. PlayHQ's finals games are used when it has them (rounds with "final" or "play-off" in their name); otherwise pick the winners on Scoreboard control.

Game night

These are the starting points; Scoreboard control can still change the court, pick a game by hand or pick another game night. On a carnival day with several rounds, "this round" moves on to each round as its first game comes up. When electronic scoring starts (10 minutes before by default; 20 or 30 when games are further apart), automatic selection switches to that game, and the scoreboard only starts asking PlayHQ for its score from then on, whether the game was picked automatically or by hand. The Socials tab's running top shooters picture is called Tournament top shooters when the fixture runs under 15 days from its first game to its last, and Season top shooters otherwise, unless you pick one.

Colours and font

Click a swatch for the colour picker, or type or paste a colour into its box as HEX (#7f4092) or RGB (127, 64, 146 or rgb(127, 64, 146)).

Brand kit colours

Paste the colours from a brand kit, PDF or website: any text with HEX or RGB values in it, such as "Purple #7F4092, RGB 127 64 146". They're saved with this competition and show in every colour picker, ready to click.

Every font is sized and centred to match Montserrat, which the graphics were drawn with: its capitals are as tall as Montserrat's. A narrow font can look small at that size; make it bigger here. Keep an eye on long team names.

Add a font file

Add a TTF, OTF, WOFF or WOFF2 file, one for each weight you have (Regular, Bold, Black…) or one variable font file. It's saved with this competition, and in its template file, so it looks the same on any computer. Then pick it in the Font box. Only add fonts you're allowed to use: open licence fonts, such as those on Google Fonts, are free to use.

Team logos

Logos are matched to PlayHQ team names by their file name ("Perth Coastals.png" for Perth Coastal), so name each file after its team. Any team without a logo shows its initials in a circle.

Teams on PlayHQ

Logos and colours belong to clubs: every team of a club, in every division, gets the club's logo and colour, and changing it on one row changes it for all of them. A team's club is its name without the division on the end ("Comets 21U", "Comets Men" and "Comets Open" are all Comets; "Stormz Div 2" is Stormz). If that's wrong for a team, pick its club in the Club box (or New club…). Pick another logo for a club whose file name doesn't match. Club colour is optional: a club with one gets it behind its teams' names on the scoreboard and behind their logo and name on The Clash; a club without one uses the competition's colours. Use the colour picker, or paste the colour as HEX or RGB.

Live viewers

Where this competition streams: any number of YouTube channels and Facebook Pages. While a video output is on, Studio adds up how many are watching every 30 seconds, for the Output page and the viewer-minutes in the sponsor report. Keys and tokens are kept on this Mac only: never in iCloud, templates or exports.

How to get a YouTube API key and a Facebook Page token

YouTube: in the Google Cloud console (console.cloud.google.com), make a project, turn on YouTube Data API v3 and make an API key under Credentials. It's free; Studio uses about 120 of its 10,000 daily units an hour on air, and 100 each time it looks for the channel's live video (at most every 5 minutes, only until it finds one). For Where, use the channel's ID (UC…), its @handle, or the live video's link.

Facebook: an admin of the Page makes an app at developers.facebook.com, and in the Graph API Explorer gets a Page access token for the Page with pages_read_engagement and pages_read_user_content (no app review is needed for your own Page; exchange it for a long-lived token so it lasts). For Where, use the Page's ID or link.

How Studio works and what every graphic does. Build the show on the Output page: each graphic's options are fields in its source's settings there. The links here, with Copy link and Preview, are for using a graphic on its own, such as an OBS browser source; the options below can be added to them by hand.

Competition setup

Every graphic is set up for one competition at a time.

Open competition setupOpen athlete photosOpen sponsors

The Sponsors page keeps the competition's sponsors by tier (Naming rights, Elite, Major, Partner, Supporter, or Platinum, Gold, Silver and Bronze, or your own names), each with its colour and white logos, tagline, website, offer, brand colour, category, deal dates and optional targets, and a QR code to its website (made on this Mac, with tracking tags for the sponsor's website analytics unless turned off; Save QR code as PNG gives a copy). A sponsor can be shown on more than one competition at its own tier in each, and is kept once. For each tier, pick what its spots do when it has no sponsor: Pull up or Pull down a sponsor from the nearest tier below or above with one, Skip tier, or Advertise vacancy with the competition's vacancy card (heading, message, contact and a link or email for its QR code; never shown without one). The Sponsor report shows what each sponsor got on screen for any nights: time on screen and where, ad plays, time with their QR code up, and progress against their targets, counted from the scene while a video output is on (Output 1, and any other output with Counts for sponsors ticked). What's on an output counts as on air only while that output is on: one that's turned off or set to Web links only isn't, even while another is. Print or save as PDF to send it to them.

Sponsors on the graphics: the naming rights sponsor (or what its empty-tier rule gives instead) is a Presented by tab over the home team on the scoreboard, a pill under the title banner and a line under the countdown. The game stats, shooting stats and top shooters cards carry the next sponsor in rotation from Major and up in their footer, the ticker leads with the next from Partner and up, and the end of night card (Up next after the last game) thanks the sponsors from Major and up with their logos (?sponsors=all every tier, ?sponsors=0 none). On any of them, ?sponsor=0 leaves the sponsor off and ?sponsor=Golds%20Bakery always shows that one; set it in the source's options on the Output page. With no sponsors they look as they always did.

The Athletes page keeps a photo of each athlete for the position match ups. Pick a team to see every athlete in its PlayHQ line ups this season and add a photo to each (or drop one onto them). Teams are listed by division, so a club with a team in more than one division (Knights) has a button in each, showing that division's line ups. Photos are saved in the competition's photos folder, named after the athlete, so they match in every game. To add many at once, drop photos named after their athletes (Jane Smith.jpg or smith_jane.png); any that match nobody can be given to an athlete in the photo library. An athlete who plays in more than one division needs only one photo (All athletes lists everyone once). Without a photo, graphics show a generic silhouette for the athlete's gender: picked on the page if you've picked one, else PlayHQ's gender for the athlete, else the PlayHQ gender of their division when they play in a men's or women's division (mixed and open divisions leave it neutral). Silhouette on each athlete shows where it came from and can override it. Games on an athlete records their games played per division (optionally per club) as of a date, and the positions they've played; PlayHQ games after that date are added on automatically.

The competition setup page keeps a saved setup for each competition (Thunder League, AMMNA Nationals, GIG WANL, or any you add) and switches between them. AMMNA Nationals and GIG WANL come with their PlayHQ link, colours and logo. AMMNA's competition is set to Australian Championships and GIG WANL's to GIG WANL, so both are ready to use. When an update improves one of these ready-made setups, yours picks up only what you haven't changed yourself. Each one has:

  • PlayHQ: the organisation's playhq.com page and which of its competitions and seasons to use. Find its competitions lists what PlayHQ has for that organisation, so you can pick the right name.
  • Names on the graphics: the competition, organisation and venue names, each blank to use PlayHQ's.
  • Game rules: quarters or halves and their length, and super shots, for the whole competition or per division. Under the super shots tick box each PlayHQ division can follow the competition, or have super shots or not. A setting can also be saved for a word instead of a whole division name, and then covers every division with that word in its name ("Open" covers "Division 1 Open"; "Men" covers "Men's" but not "Women's"); a division's own setting wins over a word. GIG WANL comes set for super shots in Open only, not 21&Under or Men's, by the words "Open", "21" and "Men". A division without super shots doesn't play the super shot animation for a +2 press or count goals as super shots, and its shooting stats and top shooters leave the Super column off. Super shots in the last [x] minutes of each quarter (default 3) is when the scoreboard shows its "Super shot" tab; 0 leaves it off.
  • Finals: the competition's finals format, and a different one for any division that needs it. Top 2: grand final, or best of three. Top 4: semis then grand final, Page (double chance for 1 and 2), or a placings day (1 v 2 and 3 v 4). Top 6: byes for 1 and 2, or a double chance for 1 and 2. Top 8: AFL style, straight knockout, or carnival placings (a knockout over three weeks with play-offs, so every team gets a place 1 to 8). Every team: everyone plays on grand final day, 1 v 2 for the title, 3 v 4 for third, 5 v 6 for fifth and so on down the ladder, with nobody knocked out (an odd team out has no game). The ladder marks the finals places by what each earns and the finals graphic follows it (see Finals below).
  • Game day: the court the scoreboard follows, whether it picks the game automatically, Electronic scoring starts [x] minutes before the game (default 10; set 20 or 30 when games are further apart), and how many days ahead counts as "this round". Automatic selection switches to the next game when its electronic scoring starts, and the scoreboard doesn't ask PlayHQ for a game's score before then, even for a game picked by hand: the control page says when it will start checking. That keeps PlayHQ's "game could not be found or was not electronically scored" error out of the debug log.
  • Colours and font: nine colours, how see-through the dark panels are, and the font, with a preview. Click a colour's swatch for a colour picker (shade square, hue slider, HEX and RGB boxes, and on Chrome or Edge Pick from screen), or type or paste a colour into its box as HEX (#7f4092) or RGB (127, 64, 146). Under Brand kit colours, paste any text from a brand kit with HEX or RGB values in it; those colours are saved with the competition and show in every picker, one click to use.
  • Font: the Font box lists the fonts that come with Studio first (they work on any computer, with no internet), then fonts added to the competition, then every font installed on this Mac (including any you add in Font Book), such as Helvetica Neue, Avenir Next, Futura and DIN Condensed. A Mac font shows only on this Mac: a graphic opened on another computer uses Montserrat instead. Add a font file takes TTF, OTF, WOFF or WOFF2 files (one per weight, or one variable font); they're saved with the competition and go in its template file, so it looks the same on any Mac. Add only fonts you're allowed to use, such as open licence fonts from Google Fonts. Any letter a font lacks comes from Montserrat. Every font is sized and centred to match Montserrat, which the graphics were drawn with, so text fills its boxes the same whichever font you pick; narrow fonts stand a little taller so they don't look small. Text size (80 to 130%) makes a font other than Montserrat bigger or smaller on every graphic; keep an eye on long team names.
  • Logos: the competition logo, in a light mode version (for a light picture behind it) and a dark mode version (for a dark one; if you add only one, it is used for both), and the team logos. The logos page and title use the light mode logo unless ?mode=dark is added; the ladder's corner, on its coloured header, uses the dark mode one unless ?mode=light is added. Drop team logo files on the page; they match clubs by file name, and the PlayHQ team list shows which logo each team gets, with a box to pick a different one.
  • Clubs: a team's logo and colour belong to its club, so every team of a club in every division shares them, and changing one row changes them all. The club is the team's name without the division on the end ("Comets 21U", "Comets Men" and "Comets Open" are all Comets; "Stormz Div 2" is Stormz). The Club box in the team list links a team to a different club, or to a new one.
  • Club colours: in the same PlayHQ team list, Add colour gives a club its own colour. The scoreboard puts it behind its teams' names and The Clash behind their logo and name, with dark or white text, whichever reads better. Clubs without one (or after ×) use the competition's colours. The match up page always uses the competition's colours. Pick the colour with the colour picker or paste it as HEX or RGB.
  • More: the PlayHQ Client ID most competitions don't need, and exporting, saving or deleting the competition. (The emoji either side of the title banner and The Clash's big word are options on those graphics on the Output page.)

Use this one switches every graphic, the control page and the Stream Deck to that competition: the round goes back to automatic and the scoreboard starts on that competition's first game. Saving the competition in use reloads the graphics with the changes. Setups are kept in the competitions folder (see Saved settings and git below).

Carnivals (like the AMMNA nationals) play several rounds a day: on those days "this round" moves on to each round as its first game comes up, and Up next looks across the whole day.

Sits centred at the bottom, just above the score ticker with a small gap, so both can be on together. ?position=top-center, bottom-center, top-left or bottom-left moves it.

Super shot tab: in a division with super shots, when the quarter clock gets to the last 3 minutes (or is already under them when the game is picked), a small yellow "Super shot" tab rises out from behind the bar above the clock, with a pulsing dot and a light sweeping across it now and then. It sinks away when the quarter's clock runs out or is reset for the next quarter. It runs off the scoreboard's own clock, so it follows the PlayHQ scorer's clock when the scoreboard does. The minutes are set per competition on the competition setup page (Game rules, 0 leaves the tab off), and ?supershot=0 leaves it off this source.

Output (the Studio scene)

Open the Output pagePreview

The Output page builds Studio's own scene: sources drawn as layers on one 1920 × 1080 frame, top layer first, and sends it to a display, a Blackmagic device or NDI® for your switcher. Each section folds away with the arrow by its title (or a click on the title), and stays folded on that Mac until it's opened again; buttons in a folded section's title row, like Output on, still work. Longer explanations wait behind Expand for info.

  • Sources: Add page adds any Studio graphic or web address; Add image adds a PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP or SVG, and Add video an MP4, WebM or MOV (saved in the data folder's media folder). Each opens a box to pick it. A new source goes above the selected one (on top if none is selected), hidden, and is selected so you can set it up straight away. Pages not in the scene yet are listed first, and Add all unused pages adds every one of them, hidden. Drag a source up or down the list to move it between layers, or use ↑ and ↓.
  • Export sources saves the scene's sources as a .zip, with the images and videos they use (untick Export with their images and videos to leave them out), to keep as a backup or move to another Mac. Import sources replaces every source in the scene with the ones in an exported .zip (or its scene.json), after asking. Imported sources start hidden and keep their Stream Deck keys. Their images and videos go in the media folder; a file already there with the same name but different contents isn't overwritten, the imported one is kept as name (2) instead. Exported without media, the files have to be in the other Studio's media folder already; the Output page lists any that are missing.
  • Page options: a Studio graphic's link options described on this page (position, heading, division, round, animate away and so on) show as fields under its Address, and are saved in the address.
  • On / Off fades a source in or out with its own Fade time in milliseconds (0 is a cut).
  • Position and scale place it on the frame; Full frame fits it to 1920 × 1080. Page size is the size a page is drawn at (1920 × 1080 for full-frame graphics).
  • Load the page fresh each time it's shown (on by default) reloads a page as it fades in so its animation replays, and unloads it when hidden. Untick it to keep a page running so it shows instantly. Refresh page reloads it now.
  • Animations that animate away switch themselves off: once a graphic has animated away and left the frame clear, its source is switched off in the scene (the Sources list and its Stream Deck key show it off), rather than staying on as an empty layer. That's The Clash and the winner animation with Animate away after set, a person lower third with Animate away after, positions with At the end: Animate away, the logo animation and the break ad with At the end: Animates away (their default), and the super shot and hot streak animations when they play once. Pages that stay on screen or wait for something to happen (super shots and hot streaks playing by themselves, the countdown, the scoreboard) stay on. This needs a scene window open (the Output page's Live View, its pop-out or a display or DeckLink output) to see the animation finish.
  • Preview opens the selected source on its own in a new window, the way the Preview buttons on this page do; the small PVW button beside a source's On / Off does the same from the list. A preview opens at half size (960 x 540): the page is still laid out at 1080p, just scaled down, on a checkerboard so the see-through parts show, so a few fit on one screen. Resize the window to make it bigger.
  • Videos start from the beginning each time they're shown (untick to carry on), can loop, and fade out when they finish unless looping. A video that fades out when it finishes is switched off in the scene, so the Sources list and its Stream Deck key show it off and the next On plays it again. Sound turns a video's sound on or off, with a volume; it plays through the Mac's audio output. Restart video starts it again.
  • ProRes: the pages can't play ProRes, so a .mov added to the media folder is converted to a WebM beside it (keeping a ProRes 4444 clip's transparency; the Output page warns when a .mov has none to keep, such as H.264), and sources using it switch over when it's done. The original is kept. The Mac app includes ffmpeg to do this (running from a copy of the code instead, run vendor/ffmpeg/build.sh once or brew install ffmpeg). MP4 and WebM play as they are.
  • Media folder: the Output page's Media folder (at the bottom, folded until you open it) lists every song, video and image added (under Music, Add image or video, or on the Sponsors page), with its length, size and where it's used: sources, page themes (?theme=), sequence music (in any competition), playlists, the super shot song, the music player and sponsors' ads. Remove deletes a file for good (with a levelled song's original) after asking, and says where it's used first. A file on air (a source showing it, or the song playing) can't be removed until it's off. Playlists, the super shot song and sponsors' ads let go of it; sources, page themes and sequence music keep its name, shown as (missing), so their settings and Stream Deck keys stay until you pick another file. A .mov and the WebM converted from it are separate files.
  • Stream Deck plugin: the Mac app includes a FirstPass Studio plugin for the Stream Deck app. Install it from the menu bar icon (Install the Stream Deck plugin) or the Output page's button. Its Source key shows or hides the source you pick in its settings (or fades it in, fades it out, restarts it, or turns a video's sound on or off) and lights up in its output's colour while the source is on, whether it was turned on from the key, the Output page or a link. With more than one output, each key's Output setting picks the output it acts on and follows (a removed output's key says no output). It shows the source's name, keeps working when you rename it, and says off air while Studio isn't running. Its Scene key goes to a saved scene, and its Sequence key plays a sequence (or presses Next, Pause or Stop), lights up while it plays and counts down the step on screen.
  • Stream Deck links: /deck/layer/<name>/on, off, toggle and restart for each source, /deck/layer/<name>/sound/on, off and toggle for a video, named in lower case with dashes for spaces (Up Next is up-next), and /deck/layers/off to hide everything. The Output page copies each source's links.
  • Sequences: play the scene's sources one after another, for the run-up to a game, a quarter break or full time. + New sequence on the Output page opens a list of steps: each step turns on the sources you pick and stays for a set time (seconds), until it finishes (its videos end, or pages that switch themselves off when done; it moves on after the video's length plus 3 s, or a minute for a page, if the end never comes), until the game moves on (the clock starts, or the next break or full time) or until I press Next. As a step ends, sources the next step doesn't use go off with their own fades. A step can also be a scene (+ Add scene: a starting or reset point; what the scene turns on stays on under the next steps and after the sequence; it goes straight on or holds like any step) or another sequence (+ Add sequence: its steps play there, so a mini sequence is built once and used in many; a sequence can't go inside itself). A sequence can be all sources, all scenes, all sequences or a mix. Tick Hide to keep one off the list (a mini sequence used inside others); it still plays inside them, when its start comes and from the Stream Deck, and Show hidden brings the hidden ones back to the list. Starts is by hand, N minutes before the picked game's scheduled start (only if Studio was running at that moment: opened later, the sequence is left for you to play by hand), at the breaks you tick (quarter, half and three quarter time) or at full time, a few seconds after the siren (from the scoreboard's clock, the PlayHQ scorer's or yours; no extra PlayHQ calls). While it waits to start its card says Starting with Start now and Cancel. Automatic sequences on above the cards turns all of that off and on (remembered). Repeat plays it once, until stopped, until play resumes or until N minutes before the start (it finishes the step on screen, then ends), and Then goes to a scene, turns off what it turned on, or leaves everything as it is. A sequence only turns off sources it turned on itself. While one plays, its card shows a dot per step, the step's time left and the whole sequence's Total (at least when part of it waits for Next, the game or a page; This time round when it repeats), with Next (end the step now), Pause and Stop (ends it and does its Then). Going to a scene, playing another sequence or hiding everything stops it without its Then; turning the step's source off by hand moves to the next step. One plays at a time, it keeps going with the Output page closed, and nothing is playing after Studio restarts. Sequences belong to the competition in use (saved in its folder as sequences.json) and change with Use this one; the sources and scenes are shared. Stream Deck: /deck/sequence/<name> plays one (/toggle stops it on a second press, /stop stops it), /deck/sequence/next, pause and stop act on whatever is playing (/deck/sequence/advance is Next only when the step on now is Until I press Next, and does nothing otherwise), /deck/sequence/now and cancel on one about to start, and /deck/sequence/auto/on, off or toggle set the switch. The break lengths (Competitions setup, Game rules) let the editor say how many times a break sequence fits in each break.
  • Music: the Output page's Music player plays songs from the media folder (MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV or AIFF; use music you're licensed to stream, as Apple Music songs are copy-protected), one song or a playlist (shuffled or not, starting again after the last or not). Add a song with Even out its loudness ticked levels it to the same loudness as the others and keeps the original in the media folder's originals. It plays wherever video sound plays (the outputs, or the Scene View while Video output is off), never twice. Play fades in over the fade in time; Pause and Stop fade out over the fade out time (slower by default); Duck (dip it low, keeping it running) goes down over the fade out time and back up over the fade in time (or half of each: a setting); Mute keeps it running. While a video with its sound on plays, the music fades out and pauses, then fades back in from where it was (or lowers, or carries on: a setting). A sequence can bring its own music: a song or playlist starting at one of its steps, from a start mark (type it, or Listen and press Mark here), with a fade in; it fades out so it's gone as the sequence ends (when its last step has a set time; ended early, it fades from then), or cuts as it ends, or carries on into the next sequence when the sequence's Then plays another one (Run-up then Walk out share one song). A sequence with no music of its own plays the music of a sequence inside it while that one plays, from its step, ending as that sequence's music ends. Going to a scene or another sequence fades a sequence's music out over its Music ends time (or cuts it, if set to cut). Tick Quiet the music on a scene (Edit scenes) or a sequence for an interview on court: whatever music is playing (the player's or a sequence's) ducks and keeps playing until the next scene, sequence or sequence step without the tick. A super shot song (a music setting, with its own volume) cuts in, no fade, when the super shot period starts in a division with super shots and fades out when the period ends. Stream Deck: /deck/music/toggle, play, pause, resume, stop, fade, next, prev, /deck/music/mute/toggle, /deck/music/duck/toggle, /deck/music/load/<song> and /deck/music/playlist/<name> (?play starts it too); ?fade=2 on any of them fades over 2 seconds, ?fade=0 cuts.
  • Mics: live mics plugged into the Mac (wireless receivers for effects mics on court or crowd mics, directly or through an audio interface) go out with the music and video sound. Add each one in the Output page's Mics section: a name, the input (Find inputs lists the Mac's, in the Studio app) and the channel (both mixed to one, or left or right, so two receivers on one stereo input are two mics). Studio mixes them into the sound of Output 1 only while it's on, and never in the Scene View, so a speaker near a mic can't feed back. The first time, the Mac asks whether FirstPass Studio may use the microphone. Each mic has a level (100% as it comes in, up to 200%), a mute, a Low cut for wind and rumble (on by default) and a meter; Level and Mute all are over all of them, and a limiter stops a shout clipping the stream. Like the music's duck, the mics dip to the Dip to level (30% by default) while music plays, while a video with its sound on plays, and while a scene or sequence with Quiet the music is on (each a tick box), going down over 1 second and back up over 2; Dip dips them by hand. A mic marked Has an external mixer never dips, since the mixer does the fading, and stays at its level. A device that isn't plugged in says so on its card. A mic can be plugged into the switcher instead (an ATEM Mini's Mic 1 or Mic 2): pick Plugged into: The switcher and its input. The switcher mixes it, and Studio moves its fader for the same level, mutes and dips (fading in small steps), changing nothing else on it: set its On, EQ and dynamics in ATEM Software Control. Moving its fader on the switcher or in ATEM Software Control makes that the mic's level, and Studio takes the faders as it finds them when it connects. Left and Right need the input split into two mono channels in ATEM Software Control. These mics are heard whatever Studio's outputs are doing. Stream Deck: /deck/mics/mute/toggle (on, off), /deck/mics/dip/toggle, /deck/mics/up and /deck/mics/down (10% a press, ?by=5 for another step), /deck/mics/level/80, and one mic by name or number: /deck/mic/court-mic/mute/toggle, /deck/mic/1/up, /deck/mic/1/down.
  • Scene View: the Output page shows the scene as it is right now (your switcher decides whether it's on air). It runs the scene's pages a second time, so untick Live View when the Mac is busy. The Scene View plays the scene's sound only while Video output is off (for testing); with a display, Blackmagic or NDI output on, the output carries the sound and the Scene View is silent, so it never plays twice. Pop out shows the Scene View in its own window to resize, move to another screen or make full screen while testing (with the same sound), with a floating Sources list to show or hide each source from there; the Output page's own Scene View turns off while it's open and comes back when it closes.
  • Display output (Mac app): sends the scene full screen to the Mac's extra displays (HDMI into a switcher). Chroma puts it over a solid colour on one display (green, blue or any colour) for a switcher that keys on that colour. Fill and key uses two extra displays: fill is the scene over black, key is white where the scene shows and black where it's see-through, for the switcher's fill and key inputs. It's only offered with two extra displays connected (a plain M1 drives one; Pro and Max chips drive more). Set those displays to exactly 1920 × 1080 (not HiDPI or scaled) in System Settings, Displays, or the scene looks soft; the Output page warns when they aren't. Turn the switcher's Pre Multiplied Key on for clean edges (with the switcher set up, Into the switcher as checks it and can set it). Each output opens full screen in its own window; leave full screen (green button or ⌃⌘F) to move it to another display or use other apps. While the output is on, its settings are locked: turn it off to change them. Output on/off, Esc on an output window, closing it, or /deck/output/on, off and toggle show or blank the displays without changing the setup.
  • Blackmagic output (Mac app): sends the scene out of a Blackmagic UltraStudio or DeckLink: fill and key with the device's external keyer (fill on one SDI out, key on the other) or fill only, in the video format you pick (1080p only; pick the one the switcher runs at, usually 1080p50). The fill is premultiplied: turn the switcher's Pre Multiplied Key on for clean edges (clip and gain aren't used then), and tick Straight only if the switcher can't do that. It needs Blackmagic Desktop Video installed (the Output page says if it isn't, with where to get it; quit and reopen Studio after installing) and the device free, so quit any other app using it first. Output on/off and the /deck/output links work the same.
  • NDI® output (Mac app): sends the scene over the network as NDI sources, in the video format you pick (1080p or 720p, 25 to 60 fps): fill and key as two sources (Source name Fill and Source name Key) for a switcher's fill and key inputs, chroma as one source over a solid colour (the same colour setting as the chroma display), or with transparency as one source with alpha for receivers that key on NDI's own transparency. They show up on the network as this Mac's name with the Source name in brackets (FirstPass Studio unless you change it). Each source only sends while something is receiving it, and the Output page says how many are. It needs NDI's runtime for Mac installed (free, the libNDI installer from ndi.link/NDIRedistV6Apple; Studio also uses the copy inside NDI Tools' apps if it finds one, as long as that copy is signed by NDI's makers, Vizrt or NewTek: a library runs inside Studio, so an unsigned file by that name is left alone and the log says so); the Output page says if it isn't, with where to get it, and finds it as soon as it's installed. Use a wired network: each 1080p source is roughly 100 to 150 Mbit/s. Sound plays through the Mac's audio output, not over NDI. Output on/off and the /deck/output links work the same. NDI® is a registered trademark of Vizrt NDI AB.
  • More than one output (Mac app): Add an output sends different graphics to up to four outputs at once, for example the downstream key on the Blackmagic and an upstream key on a camera over NDI, so those graphics switch with the camera. Each output is set up like the first (a chroma display, fill and key displays, the Blackmagic device or NDI) and has a name, a number and a colour: 1 orange, 2 pink, 3 blue, 4 green. Two outputs can't share a display, the Blackmagic device (one output) or an NDI source name: the earlier output keeps it and the Output page says so. Several outputs can each send NDI, each under its own source name, and two chroma outputs can go to two displays over HDMI, each with its own key colour. Output 1 is the primary: it plays all the sound and music, and the plain /deck/layer links act on it. The other outputs are always silent. Scenes and sequences are shared by every output, and each output plays its own: a click on a scene or sequence plays it on the output picked over the Scene View. Each output has its own current scene and plays one sequence at a time, so the same scene or sequence can be on two outputs at once; coloured dots on the cards show where. Hide all (under Sources) clears one output only and its label names it: the one the list shows when Show is set to one output, else the picked output. A sequence's Starts by itself on picks the output an automatic start plays on; if that output is removed, the sequence doesn't start by itself (rather than going to air on Output 1) until another is picked, and the log says so. Off Output 1 a sequence plays silently, and a Sponsor ads step plays its ads in that output's own Sponsor ad sources (named after it, and removed with it). Only Output 1 and outputs with Counts for sponsors ticked (on the output's card, off to start) go in the sponsor report, as Studio can't tell whether a camera's keyer or a commentary monitor is on the stream; time on a ticked output is listed as "Ladder (Wide camera)", and a sponsor on two outputs at once counts once. A Sponsor ads step on an output that doesn't count shows whose turn it is without taking it, so the ads on air keep their order. /deck/scene/<name> and /deck/sequence/<name> play on Output 1; /deck/output/<output>/scene/<name>, next, prev and again, and /deck/output/<output>/sequence/<name> (with /play, /toggle or /stop), next, advance, pause, stop, now and cancel play on that output. Sources are built once and shared; each output has its own on and off for every source, as coloured numbered buttons in the Sources list (a new output starts with everything off). Show above the list narrows it to one output, the tabs over the Scene View show each output, and Pop out opens the one picked. Stream Deck: /deck/output/<output>/on, off and toggle for one output (its name as a link, or its number), /deck/output/all/… for every one, and /deck/output/<output>/layer/<source>/on, off, toggle and /deck/output/<output>/layers/off for one output's sources (the Source settings list them). Each output with its own graphics runs its pages once more, so watch the Mac's load with three or four: while an output is on, its card shows its CPU (a percentage of one processor core), memory and the frame rate its pages draw at against what they should (in amber when it falls under 90%), with Studio's total and the graphics processor under the status, and the log has a Performance line once a minute.
  • Chroma key safe: a chroma key cuts out one colour, so anything half see-through (see-through panels, soft shadows and glows, fades, soft edges) mixes with that colour and keys with a fringe or holes. With Chroma (a display, or NDI's chroma signal) picked, Chroma key safe is ticked unless you untick it, and the Output page warns that the sources are switched to it: every graphic and animation goes out with solid panels, no soft shadows, glows, light rays or flashes (hard shadows stay, made solid), and nothing half see-through, so fades become cuts (sources cut instead of fading) and soft edges are hard. Options that only fade, like the countdown's fade and the ad's glow, are skipped, and the source's options say so. Titles, logos, light and dark modes and every other setting work as usual. It never changes a saved setting: pick another output, or untick it, and everything is back as it was. The Scene View shows the scene chroma key safe while it's on. The Output page also lists any competition or club colour close to the key colour (a keyer would cut holes in it); logos can't be checked, so look at those. For a page on its own (such as an OBS browser source keyed on a colour) add ?chromaKey=true to its link.
  • Switcher (a Blackmagic ATEM): the Switcher card under Video output controls an ATEM over the network: the Mac and the switcher on the same network, ideally wired. Set up, then Look for switchers, finds every ATEM on the network the way ATEM Software Control does (the first time, macOS asks to let Studio use the local network: say Allow); pick one and Studio connects and remembers it by name, so it finds it again if the venue's router gives it another address. Or type its address (as set in ATEM Setup; an ATEM ships as 192.168.10.240) works too. The card shows the switcher's inputs by the names set in ATEM Software Control, red on program and green on preview: click one to put it on preview, then Cut or Auto (the switcher's own transition) to take it. Fade to black, and each upstream and downstream key on and off (red while on air; a downstream key's Auto takes it at its own rate), are there too. Studio reads what the switcher has and offers only that:
    • ATEM Mini and Mini Pro: 4 inputs, one upstream key, one downstream key. Fill and key into the downstream key takes two inputs and leaves two cameras; a chroma key needs the upstream key (one input, three cameras left).
    • ATEM Mini Extreme: 8 inputs, four upstream keys, two downstream keys.
    A link or button for something the switcher doesn't have (a second downstream key on a Mini Pro, say) is refused with what it does have.
    Into the switcher as: each output's setup (under Video output) says which of the switcher's keys its graphics come into and which inputs its fill and key are plugged into (a chroma output needs just one). The usual key is downstream key 1 for Output 1 and upstream keys 2 to 4 for Outputs 2 to 4; a chroma output goes on an upstream key, as only those key on a colour. Pick another key, or Not keyed by the switcher. Studio then checks that key's settings in the switcher, read only, and lists what doesn't suit the output: its fill and key sources, its type (Chroma for a chroma output, Luma for fill and key) and Pre Multiplied Key (on, unless a Blackmagic device sends Straight). It also says when the output can't be keyed as it is: a chroma output on a downstream key, NDI with transparency or fill only (an ATEM needs a key signal or a colour), two outputs on the same key, or a key the switcher hasn't got. Set up this key for me changes just those settings on the switcher, after asking; it leaves the key's clip, gain, mask, position and whether it's on air alone, and the log says what it changed. For a chroma key, pick the key colour in ATEM Software Control with its sample picker. Nothing is sent to the switcher until it's pressed.
    Cameras per output: an output on an upstream key can say which cameras its graphics go with (ticked under Into the switcher as; the outputs' own fill and key inputs aren't offered). Studio's cuts, autos and stingers, from the Switcher card, a Stream Deck key or a sequence or scene, then take that key on with those cameras and off with the others in the same transition, so the camera and its graphics change on the same frame (Studio picks the keys in the switcher's next transition and puts the BKGD and KEY buttons back as they were afterwards). A straight-to-program press brings the key straight after. With Keys follow the camera on (the Switcher card, on unless turned off), a cut made on the switcher itself or in ATEM Software Control brings the keys a frame or two later. It only acts on a change of camera, so a key turned on or off by hand stays as it is, and it sends nothing on connecting. An output with no cameras ticked, on a downstream key (over every camera), or whose key check shows a problem is left alone. The output's card says On air with Bench or Waiting for Bench, and the Switcher card underlines each key in its output's colour. The switcher's own buttons, ATEM Software Control and other controllers keep working alongside Studio, and the card follows what they do. Studio sends nothing to the switcher by itself: not when it connects, and not when it reconnects. If the switcher stops answering (a pulled cable, a power cut), or is off when Studio connects, the card turns red within about five seconds and Studio keeps trying; anything pressed meanwhile is refused, not saved up for later, and the log says so. Studio connects when it goes on air and lets go when it goes off air. Stream Deck: /deck/switcher/cut, auto and ftb; /deck/switcher/preview/<input> and program/<input> (its number, or its name such as Camera%202); /deck/switcher/usk/<n>/on, off or toggle; /deck/switcher/dsk/<n>/on, off, toggle or auto; and /deck/switcher/macro/<n> (a macro saved on the switcher, numbered from 1 as in ATEM Software Control). The Stream Deck plugin's Switcher key presses any of these and shows the tally: a camera's key lights red while it's on program and green on preview, a key or Fade to black red while on air, and every key goes dark with a yellow dot while the switcher isn't answering.
    Stinger: with the Stinger page as a source in the scene, the card's Stinger button (and /deck/switcher/stinger, /deck/switcher/stinger/<input>) plays it on the output on a downstream key (Output 1, unless another output that's switched on is Into the switcher as a downstream key, as only a downstream key covers the whole picture) and cuts to the preview input underneath it. The stinger tells Studio when it starts and when it covers the frame, from its own settings, and the cut lands in the middle of the cover (0.72 s in with the usual half second hold), so the cut comes about a second and a half after the press, once the page has loaded. That output's picture reaches the switcher a few frames late (more over NDI than HDMI): set its line-up in frames (each output keeps its own, so moving the stinger to another output keeps both) (−25 to 25, at the switcher's own frame rate) to move the cut, and press Test the line-up before you're live: it cuts just 2 frames after the bands cover the frame, so if the old picture flashes before them, add frames (run it two or three times and go by what you see each time: one run can be a frame out either way). Only that output's own video output starts the timing, not a preview or another output, so a stinger needs that output on. With no stinger in the scene, a stinger is an auto; if the page doesn't start within 3 s, it's a plain cut, and the log says which. A second stinger while one plays is left out (a press, a Stream Deck key, or a sequence or scene's own Stinger to), so a double tap never makes two cuts. Stinger to a camera that's already on program plays the stinger with no cut (for a change of graphics under it). In a sequence or scene, actions at the same moment as a stinger wait for its cut, so a key change lands under the bands too.
    Switcher actions in sequences and scenes: once a switcher is set up, a sequence's sources or scene step has a 🎥 button, and each scene has 🎥 Switcher under Edit scenes. Each adds up to eight actions: Cut to, Auto to or Stinger to an input (or what's on preview), Put on preview, an Upstream key or Downstream key on, off or toggled (a downstream key can go at its rate), Fade to black or back, and Run macro, each as the step starts or the scene is picked, or up to 60 s in (in tenths of a second). Actions at the same moment go in order, so a preview then a cut works. A step's actions still to come are called off when the step ends or the sequence stops; a scene's when another scene is picked. A scene step does the scene's actions too. They happen only when the scene or sequence plays on Output 1 (its picture is the one going into the switcher); on another output it plays without touching the switcher. The editor offers the inputs and keys the connected switcher has; one it hasn't got (upstream key 2 on a Mini Pro, say) is shown in red and skipped, with a line in the log. If the switcher isn't answering when an action's moment comes, it's skipped and logged, never done late. Scenes and sequences with switcher actions show 🎥 by their name.

The scene only changes when you change it, so it adds no PlayHQ requests beyond those of the pages in it.

Control page settings (every page follows these)

Every graphic uses the round, game and court set on the scoreboard control page. A setting added to a page's own link (like ?division=2 or ?round=6) overrides it for that page only.

  • Round: Automatic picks the round played in the next 5 days, counting today, so a Tuesday round is picked from the Friday before (the number of days is on the competition setup page). Otherwise it's the last round played. On a day with several rounds, it moves on to each round as its first game comes up. Pick a round from the list to test with it, then set it back to Automatic.
  • Always a game on: when Studio starts, or the scoreboard is cleared, it puts a game on straight away with Follow PlayHQ on: the game for now on the court, otherwise this round's first game on that court, otherwise this round's first game. The board is only left empty if PlayHQ can't be reached or the competition isn't found (check the PlayHQ link and competition on the competition setup page).
  • Game: tick Pick the game automatically for court and set the court (it starts on the court set on the competition setup page, e.g. Indoor 01 or just 1). It shows the first game of the day on that court, then switches to each next game when its electronic scoring starts (10 minutes before by default, set on the setup page), with the quarter back to Q1 and a full clock. A final (a round with "Final" in its name) stays on until PlayHQ marks it finished, in case of extra time. With a round picked by hand, it follows that round's games on the court by their own date and time: the game due now, otherwise the next one to come, otherwise (a round already played) its last game.
  • Overrides: picking a game from the list turns automatic selection off until you tick it again or change the court. Pressing a score button switches scores to manual, and any clock button switches the clock to manual.
  • Follow PlayHQ: the score and quarter come from PlayHQ's live scoring every 5 seconds, and Follow the PlayHQ scorer's clock runs the clock from the scorer's clock too. Switching scores between Follow PlayHQ and Manual (on the control page, the Stream Deck, by pressing a score button or by picking a game) turns the clock option on or off to match, and the control page says so. Changing the clock after that still wins: untick the box or press a clock button to run the clock by hand while scores follow PlayHQ.
  • Pages that use the picked game's division: ladder and finals (without ?division=), ladder bar, top shooters, title, match up, The Clash, winner celebration, team lists, shooting stats and full time. The fixture, results, ticker and up next use the round.
  • Stream Deck: /deck/auto/on, /deck/auto/off, /deck/auto/toggle, /deck/court/2, /deck/clock/playhq/on and /deck/ticker/faster / slower. The Stream Deck page has the rest, with Copy buttons and the plugin. A score key switches scoring to Manual (Follow PlayHQ stops for the score and the clock), and a clock key stops the clock following the PlayHQ scorer.

Before you go on air

  1. Open the app. The Output page opens and Studio goes on air. Keep a Studio window open all night; closing the last one takes it off air.
  2. Right competition. Competitions setup shows the one in use at the top. Check its PlayHQ link, the court to follow and the scoring window (Electronic scoring starts N minutes before the game).
  3. Right game. On Scoreboard control, the picked game is tonight's first on your court and Follow PlayHQ is on. If the round is wrong, force this round there.
  4. Scores arrive. Once the scorer has started on PlayHQ, the control page says the game is being live scored and the scoreboard moves. Nothing is read before the scoring window opens, so a quiet board before then is normal.
  5. Scene and output. On the Output page turn on the display, NDI or Blackmagic output your switcher takes, check the fill and key or chroma setting, and show one graphic to see it land.
  6. Sound. Play a few seconds of music and an animation with sound, and check the preview sound only comes out where you want it.
  7. Stream Deck. Press a Source key and watch the Output page's Sources list follow it. A score key switches scoring to Manual, so put those back to Follow PlayHQ after testing.
  8. Sponsors. Spots and ads for tonight are on; the exposure report starts counting when the output turns on.

If PlayHQ stops answering the scoreboard keeps its last score, the ladder, fixtures and game list keep what they last read, and the debug log records each failed read. Switch to Manual on Scoreboard control (or press a score key) and run the board by hand; Follow PlayHQ picks it up again when it is back. If Studio itself stops, the 1P menu's Restart Studio brings it back with the scores and clock it had.

How Studio is put together, module by module, is in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md in the repository.

Mac app

FirstPass Studio is a Mac app for Apple silicon Macs (M1 and later). Open it from Applications or Spotlight and the 1P icon appears in the menu bar, off air. Choose Open Studio (or any page) there to put Studio on air; the Output page opens in the app's own window.

Studio only runs while a Studio window is open. Opening a window (Open Studio or any page in the 1P menu, ⌘N) puts Studio on air: it serves the graphics and the Stream Deck links, talks to PlayHQ, and restarts by itself if it stops. Closing the last Studio window takes it off air, so nothing talks to PlayHQ and the graphics and Stream Deck get no answer until a window opens again. Closing the last window while on air asks first (Take Off Air or Cancel), and so does ⌘Q, so a stray keypress can't stop a stream. Keep a window open, such as the Output page, for the whole stream.

The 1P menu opens Studio, the control and setup pages, the settings folder and the debug log, and has Stop Studio (while on air: closes the Studio windows and goes off air), Restart Studio, Open at login and Check for updates. The line under the version says whether Studio is on air or off air. The app always starts in the menu bar off air, whether opened at login (tick Open at login) or by hand, so nothing talks to PlayHQ until you open Studio. Updates download by themselves and install when the app quits, never mid-stream.

The app window is like any Mac app's: no address bar, tabs or bookmarks, the Studio header is the title bar (drag it to move the window), and it shows in the Dock and ⌘Tab while a window is open. Links that open a new tab on Studio (a preview, the control or setup page) open another window; links elsewhere (PlayHQ, GitHub) open in your browser. The menu at the top of the screen has File > Open in Browser (⇧⌘O) and Copy Page Link (⇧⌘C) for the page in front, Go for each Studio page (⌘1 to ⌘7), View for reload, back and zoom, New Window (⌘N), and Close Studio Windows (⌥⌘W) in the FirstPass Studio menu.

Building and releasing it is in the README under Desktop app (Mac).

Links (this Mac's .local name)

The links on this page, the control page and the Mac app's menu (Copy the Studio link too) start with this Mac's Bonjour name, , instead of localhost. It is the Local hostname in System Settings > General > Sharing, so the same link works on this Mac and from a phone, another computer or a Stream Deck on the same network. The port stays 8080, and http://localhost:8080 still works on this Mac, so links and Stream Deck buttons you already have carry on. To change it, set "linkHost" in config.json in the data folder: "bonjour" (the default), "localhost", or any name or IP address, then choose Restart Studio in the 1P menu.

Anything on the same network can open these pages and press the Stream Deck links (scores, clock, game), as it always could. Stream on a network you trust, not open venue Wi-Fi, or block incoming connections to FirstPass Studio in the Mac's firewall.

Saved settings

Everything Studio saves lives in one data folder on this computer, outside the app, so updates never touch it: ~/Library/Application Support/FirstPass Studio (the setup page shows where, and Open the settings folder in the 1P menu opens it).

  • competitions/: a folder per competition with its setup (settings.json), team logos, competition logo, athlete photos and athlete register, and active.json, the one in use.
  • control.json: the control page's choices (the round picked by hand, automatic game selection and its court, following the PlayHQ clock, how often to check PlayHQ, showing the clock, the ticker speed, finals winners picked by hand).
  • scoreboard-state.json: the game on the board, the scores and the clock.
  • config.json (optional): changes this computer's settings (port, log size, how often to check PlayHQ, remoteChanges) from the app's defaults. Let other devices change things in the menu bar icon sets remoteChanges: unticked, phones, other computers and Stream Decks on the network can still open every graphic but can't change scores, the scene, sequences, settings or media, or read private data (the settings and scene backups, the athlete register, the debug log, the sponsor reports, the competition setup); Studio answers those with a 403 that says so. Either way a change must come from Studio's own pages or a program (the Stream Deck plugin, a Website key): a request a browser marks as coming from a web page on another site, or that asks for Studio by a name this computer doesn't answer to, gets a 403 too.
  • switcher.json: the ATEM switcher Studio controls (its name on the network, or the address typed) and the stinger's line-up in frames, one for the Mac whichever competition is in use.
  • app-settings.json: the Mac app's own choices (where the window was).
  • logs/: the debug log. backups/: a copy of the folder made before an update changes how settings are saved.

Keeping the settings in iCloud Drive

In the 1P menu, Settings folder > Move to iCloud Drive… asks for a folder in iCloud Drive (make one called FirstPass Studio, say) and moves the settings into a subfolder named after this Mac, its Local hostname in System Settings > General > Sharing: iCloud Drive/FirstPass Studio/Studio-MacBook-Pro. Each Mac signed in to the same Apple account gets its own subfolder, so two Studios never save over each other's settings, and a second Mac with the same name is stopped from using the first one's. Studio goes off air for the move and comes straight back. Before anything moves, a copy of the settings is saved in backups/ in the folder on this Mac, and the originals are removed only once every file has copied across. If the folder already has settings for this Mac (from before), Studio asks whether to use those or replace them.

Some files always stay on this Mac: the debug log, the scores and clock (scoreboard-state.json, saved every few seconds during a game), PlayHQ caches, the app's window positions and data-location.json, which says where the settings are. If iCloud has kept only a placeholder of a file (Optimise Mac Storage), Studio downloads it before going on air; to keep it all on the Mac, right-click the folder in Finder and choose Keep Downloaded. If the iCloud folder can't be found (iCloud Drive turned off, signed out), Studio says so and doesn't start, rather than starting with no settings. Settings folder > Move back to this Mac… moves them back.

Settings folder > Restore from another Mac… copies another Mac's settings (competitions, scenes, sequences, music, the control page's choices) over this Mac's: pick the Mac from the list of folders beside this one, or choose its folder. The other Mac keeps its own; this Mac's settings are backed up in backups/ on this Mac first, and are only replaced once the copy has checked out. If this Mac's settings are on the Mac, they go into iCloud Drive too, in a new folder for this Mac beside the other Mac's, and stay there from then on. Studio goes off air for the copy and comes straight back. Open the settings folder opens wherever they are now.

Backing up the settings

Back up… in the 1P menu saves every setting in one zip: competitions with their logos and athletes, sponsors, scenes, sequences, music and playlists, the media folder, the control page's choices and config.json, wherever they are kept (on the Mac or in iCloud Drive). The debug log, the scores and clock, PlayHQ caches and stream keys stay out. First it asks for notes (what the backup is for, say) and whether to include the media folder (videos, songs), which can make the zip big. The zip is named FirstPassStudioBackup-2026-10-24_10-46-02.zip, the date and time it was made, so each one is new and they sort oldest to newest. Beside it goes a summary with the same name, FirstPassStudioBackup-2026-10-24_10-46-02.txt: the notes, then the names of the competitions (and which is in use), sequences, playlists, sources, scenes and sponsors. Inside the zip is one folder of the same name holding the settings as they are in the settings folder, the same summary.txt, and studio-export.json saying which Mac and Studio version made it. Files iCloud hasn't downloaded yet are downloaded first. Studio stays on air while it backs up, and works off air too. Link for scripts: /api/settings/export downloads the same zip (add ?media=0 to leave media out, &notes=… for notes).

Restore… in the 1P menu puts a backup back: pick a FirstPassStudioBackup-….zip, and Studio shows when and on which Mac it was made, its notes and how many competitions, sequences, playlists, sources, scenes and sponsors it holds, then asks before replacing this Mac's settings with it (on the Mac or in iCloud Drive, wherever they are kept now). This Mac's settings are first copied to backups/ in the folder on this Mac, and only replaced once the whole backup has unpacked and checked out, so a damaged zip changes nothing. A backup made without the media folder keeps this Mac's media. The debug log, scores and clock aren't touched. Studio goes off air while the settings swap and comes straight back. A backup from an older Studio is brought up to date as Studio starts; one from a newer Studio opens with competitions read-only until this Mac's Studio is updated.

Studio starts with no competitions. On the competition setup page, make a new one or import a template file. The starter competitions (Thunder League, AMMNA Nationals, GIG WANL, West Coast Thunder) are template files in the repository's templates/ folder. Export as template saves a competition with its logos as one .json file, and Import a template file adds it on any Studio. Delete this competition works on any competition; deleting the one in use switches the graphics to another.

Changes on the competition setup page aren't kept until you press Save. With changes not saved yet, leaving the page (a tab, or the Go menu), opening another competition, Use this one, New competition or Import a template file first asks Save, Don't save or Cancel. Export as template asks to save first, since the file is made from the saved settings. Closing the window, reloading or quitting asks whether to leave without saving.

The editors that open over a page (a sponsor, a sequence or a playlist on the Output page, an athlete's Games) ask the same way: with changes not saved yet, pressing Escape or leaving the page from the Go menu asks Save, Don't save or Keep editing, and closing the window asks whether to leave without saving. Their Cancel button still just cancels.

When an update changes how settings are saved, Studio backs up the data folder and brings it up to date on its first start. Settings saved by a newer Studio open read-only in an older one.

Data and bandwidth

scoreboard.html, goals.html and countdown.html (and the control page) follow the scoreboard over the one connection Studio's pages keep open for the scene, so a score press or a PlayHQ reading reaches them the moment it happens, with no asking in between. ticker.html, viewers.html (every 15 seconds, Studio only) and the automatic supershot.html and streak.html keep asking for new data; ticker.html also asks for its speed every 3 seconds. None of that reaches PlayHQ. The super shot and hot streak pages ask Studio every second for the scoreboard and nothing else, so they add no PlayHQ requests. Every other graphic gets its data once when it loads, and again each time it's shown on the Output page (with Load the page fresh each time it's shown, the default). The Clash and the winner celebration only retry if Studio isn't running.

Pages only ever talk to Studio on this computer, which uses no internet. Studio is the only thing that contacts PlayHQ, and it caches what it gets:

  • Scoreboard following PlayHQ: one small request every 5 seconds by default (Check PlayHQ every on the control page, 1 to 120 seconds), starting when the game's electronic scoring starts (set on the setup page). Nothing is asked before then. Studio makes this request itself whatever windows are open, so the score, the goal order and the game triggers (quarter breaks, full time) keep up even while the scoreboard is hidden and the control page is closed; the pages share that reading rather than adding to it.
  • Studio scene (scene.html and the Output page): nothing. They keep one connection open to Studio, which only sends when the scene changes; the pages in the scene ask for what they normally do.
  • Display output (Mac app): nothing. The output windows show the scene page, so they ask for what the scene's pages normally ask for, once per display (fill and key means the pages run twice). The Blackmagic and NDI outputs run them once more, off screen, and each extra output runs the pages turned on for it once more. NDI's video goes over your local network only, never the internet.
  • Mics: nothing over the internet. Output 1's window tells Studio what it hears twice a second, and the Output page asks for it twice a second while its Mics section is open; the sound itself stays inside the Mac. A mic on the switcher is a few small fader messages over the switcher's connection when its level changes (about 20 a second while a dip fades).
  • Switcher (an ATEM, if one is set up): nothing over the internet. Studio keeps one connection to the switcher on your local network, a few kilobytes a second of small packets each way; looking for switchers asks the local network once.
  • Stream Deck plugin's keys: nothing. The plugin keeps one connection open to Studio while any of its keys is on screen (and one more while a Switcher key is), and Studio only sends when the scene or the switcher changes.
  • Countdown: nothing. The clock ticks in the page and the start time comes from the game already picked.
  • Live viewers (Competitions setup): only while a video output is on, one small request per stream every 30 seconds to YouTube or Facebook, plus a YouTube search when a channel's live video isn't known yet (at most every 5 minutes). Nothing when no streams are set up.
  • Last 10 goals: nothing extra. It shares the scoreboard's 5 second reading, and Studio works out the goal order from it.
  • Ticker: other courts' scores at most every 30 seconds.
  • Automatic game selection: the fixture about once a minute for the current round, every 10 minutes for the rest.
  • Athletes page: when you open a team, the line up of each of its games this season, a few at a time. Finished games are only ever read once: Studio keeps their line ups in game-cache.json in the data folder, so they're kept through restarts too.
  • Finals: the division's ladder and its season's rounds when the page loads (the rounds are kept for 10 minutes, the current one for a minute).
  • Team lists: warmed up ahead, so they show straight away. When a game is picked (by hand or automatically) and when Studio starts, it reads the game's line up and both teams' season squads, and the next game's teams on the same court too. Finished games come from game-cache.json, so after the first time that's only a few small requests per game picked. Squads are kept for 30 minutes; the team list itself asks for the game's line up and score when it loads.

Each answer is a few kilobytes, well under 1 MB an hour in total. Two options make a page poll, both off unless you add them: ?refresh= on the ladder, and &reload= on the scoreboard, scene and control page.

Something not working? The debug log is in the logs folder of the data folder, or open the debug log. Send logs/playhq-graphics.log to Claude.

On the Output page, Add page puts any of these graphics in the scene; turn each on or off there or from the Stream Deck. Animations run at the same speed at any frame rate, 50p or 60p.

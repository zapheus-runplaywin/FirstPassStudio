Sponsors that show up, and proof that they did

A sponsor library in Studio, placements across in-game, break, pre-game and post-game graphics, sponsor video ads that rotate fairly through sequences, a QR code per sponsor, live viewer numbers from YouTube and Facebook, and an exposure report to hand each sponsor after the night.

1. The sponsor library

Sponsors get their own Sponsors tab, after Competitions setup. It opens on the competition in use, like every Studio page. A new sponsor belongs to that competition; its "Also shown on" list shares it with other competitions (the same PlayHQ organisation's first) without copying it, at its own tier in each. A business that backs every Thunder League division is set up once, and its logo or link is fixed in one place.

What a sponsor holds

  • Name and a short tagline ("Fresh bread since 1987")
  • Logos: full colour, and white for dark panels (Studio makes a white one from the colour logo if none is given)
  • Brand colour, used for the card edge and QR panel
  • Website link for the QR code, plus an optional offer ("10% off with code THUNDER")
  • Ads: video ads and still ad cards from the media folder
  • Deal: start and end dates, and optional targets (seconds on screen per stream, ad plays per stream)

Tiers

Naming rights one per competition. Title reads "Thunder League presented by…", logo on the scoreboard tab, first in every rotation.

Elite sponsored moments, video ads, break cards.

Major video ads, break cards, presented-by footers on stat cards.

Partner ticker slot and rotating logo spots.

Supporter sponsor wall and end of night thank you.

Five tiers in a fixed order. Each competition names them: Names (Naming rights, Elite, Major, Partner, Supporter, the default), Metals (Naming rights, Platinum, Gold, Silver, Bronze) or its own. Tiers set the default share of screen time (5 : 3 : 2 : 1 : ½). A sponsor's own target overrides it. Every tier also shows on the sponsor wall.

If the tier it pulls from is empty too, Studio keeps going the same way, and skips once it runs out of tiers. A vacancy card only goes where a card fits (breaks and the sponsor wall). On the scoreboard tab and sponsored moments, Advertise vacancy acts as Skip tier. A Sponsor ads step with nothing to play uses the house ad, or moves straight on when the house ad is off.

The vacancy card belongs to the competition and is filled in by its admin: a heading, a short message, a contact name and a link or email for the QR code. With no link set, Advertise vacancy acts as Skip tier, so a half-finished card never goes to air.

3. Video ads

Sponsor ads are ordinary videos in the media folder, attached to a sponsor. They play through what already exists: a scene source draws them, done.js and the video's end switch them off, and sequences decide when.

New sequence step: Sponsor ads

Instead of naming one video, a step says "play 1 sponsor ad" (or 2, or "fill 60 s"). When the step starts, the rotation picks which ad, Studio plays it in a shared Sponsor ad source, and the step lasts as long as that ad. The quarter time sequence then never needs editing as sponsors come and go.

Still ads and the PIVT ad

A still ad card (a sponsor's own designed PNG) plays for a set time, 8 s by default. The existing PIVT and FirstPass Studio ad (ad.html) can join the rotation as a house ad that fills any gap when no sponsor is due.

Ads keep the current sound rules: music fades out and pauses under an ad with sound, and fades back after. A Stream Deck /deck/sponsor/ad link plays the next due ad by hand.

4. Fair rotation

One rotation decides every "rotate" placement and every Sponsor ads step, so a sponsor's share holds across graphics and ads together.

  1. Who is furthest behind their target (or their tier share) for tonight goes next.
  2. Never the same sponsor twice in a row, and a sponsor's own ads take turns.
  3. Competitors kept apart: sponsors in the same category (two physios) are never back to back. Category is an optional field.
  4. Only on air counts: time is counted while a video output is on (or, for pages used on their own, while the page is visible), so rehearsals before the stream don't use up a sponsor's share.

5. QR codes

Every sponsor with a website link gets a QR code, made locally by Studio (a small QR library bundled with the app, no web service). It shows on the sponsor card, can be a page option on the sponsor wall and Up next, and can be saved as a PNG for the sponsor's own use.

  • Tracking tags added for the sponsor: the link gets utm_source=livestream&utm_medium=qr&utm_campaign=thunder-league&utm_content=quarter-time, so the sponsor sees scans from the stream in their own website analytics. Studio can't count scans itself without a web server in the middle; that could come later as a FirstPass short link.
  • Readable on a phone watching a phone: at least 220 px on the 1080 frame, white panel with a full quiet border, shown for at least 8 s. Studio warns when a link is so long the code gets too dense, and suggests the sponsor's shorter link.
  • The same QR on the sponsor's poster at the venue gives one link for court-side and stream.

6. Live viewer numbers

Both platforms give a live viewer count, and Studio can read it the same way it reads PlayHQ: the helper on the Mac polls while on air and pages only talk to the helper. An MCP server isn't the right tool here; MCP is how Claude reaches a service, not how a running app does. Studio calls the platforms directly with a key or token Matt sets once, and keeps it in the Mac's keychain, never in the settings folder that can sync to iCloud.

YouTube straightforward

The YouTube Data API returns concurrentViewers for a live video (videos.list, part liveStreamingDetails). A free API key from Google Cloud is enough for a public stream; no sign-in.

Studio finds tonight's live video from the channel once (a search that costs 100 of the 10,000 free daily units), or Matt pastes the stream link. Polling every 30 s for a 4 hour night costs about 480 units.

The count is missing if the channel hides live viewer counts.

Facebook more setup

A Page's live video has a live_views field, "the instant viewer count of the live video now". Reading it needs a Page access token, which means a free Meta developer app with Matt as its admin and a one-time "Connect Facebook" sign-in from Studio.

An app used only by its own admins works without Meta's App Review. A long-lived Page token doesn't expire unless the password or permissions change. I'll confirm the exact permissions against Matt's Page while building.

7. Exposure report (proof of play)

This is the piece that sells the next season. Studio logs every time a sponsor is on air: which placement, from when to when, and the viewer count at the time. After the night, a report page shows each sponsor what they got, and saves as a PDF to email them.

8. Marketing ideas

Things the Studio features above make easy to sell. Only the first three need anything beyond this design.

Sponsor packages

A one-page rate card generated from the library: what each tier gets (placements, seconds per stream, ad plays) and last season's audience from the report. Clubs can hand it straight to local businesses.

Scan to win

A sponsor giveaway at a break: "Scan to win a $100 voucher". The QR links to the sponsor's form, so the sponsor gets leads they can count. It is the strongest reason to put up a QR at all.

Sponsor clips for socials

Studio records the sponsor's moments (super shots they own, the quarter time card) as short clips the sponsor can post. Sponsors value content they can share as much as airtime.

Own a moment

Sell each recurring moment once a season: super shots, player of the match, goal of the quarter, the last 2 minutes. Clear, cheap to deliver, and easy for a business to picture.

Club and player sponsors

A club's own sponsor on its team list and match up card, sold by the club and shown by Studio. That spreads sponsorship work to clubs instead of the association alone.

Offer codes

A stream-only code ("THUNDER10") on the card gives the sponsor sales they can count, even when nobody scans.

9. Build order

Each step is its own PR, usable on its own. Only the first carries the minor version label.

  1. The Sponsors tab. The library, tiers and their names, empty-tier rules, the vacancy card, logos, link, offer, deal dates, QR preview and Save QR as PNG. Data version bump.
  2. Sponsor card and sponsor wall. sponsor.html and sponsorwall.html with rotation, on the Graphics page, Output page page options and examples. MP4 preview.
  3. Sponsors on existing graphics. Naming rights on title, countdown and scoreboard tab; ticker slot; presented-by footers; end of night strip.
  4. Sponsor ads in sequences. Ads on a sponsor, the Sponsor ads step, fair rotation, the house ad, /deck/sponsor/ad and a Stream Deck key.
  5. Exposure report. On-air log, report page per night and season, saved as PDF, targets shown on the setup list.
  6. Viewer numbers. YouTube first, then Facebook, on the Output page and in the report.
  7. Sponsored moments. Presented-by tags on super shot, streaks and moments.

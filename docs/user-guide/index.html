Chapter 1 of 11

What Studio is

FirstPass Studio is a Mac app that draws broadcast graphics for a netball livestream and sends them to your vision switcher. The scores, clocks, ladders, fixtures and line-ups come from PlayHQ's public data, so there is nothing to type in during the game.

In this chapter
  1. 1The words used in this guide
  2. 2The pages you work in
  3. 3The sample competition in this guide

Studio does one job on stream night: graphics. Your cameras, switcher and streaming encoder stay as they are. Studio hands the switcher a picture with transparency (fill and key, or a chroma colour) so the graphics sit over the vision.

The words used in this guide

Studio
The whole thing: the Mac app, the pages you work in, and the graphics it puts on screen.
On air, off air
Studio is on air while a Studio window is open. On air it talks to PlayHQ, serves the graphics and answers the Stream Deck. Closing the last window takes it off air.
A graphic, or a page
One thing that can go on screen: the scoreboard, a ladder, a lower third, an animation. Each is a web page that Studio draws.
A source
A graphic placed in the scene, with its own position, options and fade. The scoreboard is one source; a ladder is another.
The scene
Studio's own 1920 × 1080 frame, built from sources switched on and off. It is what the switcher receives.
A scene (on the Scenes list)
A saved look: which sources are on and which are off. "Live game" and "Clear" are scenes.
A sequence
A list of steps played in order from one press: a run-up, a quarter-time break, a full-time wrap.
The competition
Everything that belongs to one league: its PlayHQ link, names, rules, colours, logos, sponsors and athletes. Studio keeps a setup per competition and uses one at a time.
The data folder
Where Studio saves everything, outside the app, so updates never touch it.

The pages you work in

Every Studio window has the same bar at the top, with the FirstPass Studio mark and the tabs in three groups by what you are doing. Run is the night itself: Output, where the show is built and sent out, and Scoreboard control, which runs the board. Plan is before the night: the Production Planner plans the court and the commentators, and Socials makes the night's pictures for social media. Set up is done once: Recordings, Competitions setup, Sponsors, Athletes and Devices. Docs sits on its own at the right: it is this guide, with the bar on top. In the app, ⌘1 to ⌘9 and ⌘0 go to Output, Production Planner, Scoreboard control, Recordings, Socials, Competitions setup, Sponsors, Athletes, Devices and Docs.

The right of the bar carries the status chips, on every page, so you can see the night from wherever you are: ON AIR in orange like a tally light, with a dot per output that is solid in the output's colour while it sends and hollow while it doesn't (or Off air); the game on the board, its dot orange before the clock has started, green from the first clock start or score until an hour after the game ends, then grey; PlayHQ, whose dot is the link alone (green when PlayHQ answers, amber when a reading is overdue, red when it keeps failing, grey when scores are by hand) and whose words give the quarter and clock, or game completed once PlayHQ closes the game (full time when the last quarter is run down by hand); the sequence playing with its step and the time left on it (and what the run order plays after it), the triggered sequence counting down to start or the run order's Next, ATEM while a switcher is set up (green when it answers, red when it doesn't), and REC while a game records (grey while Record games waits for one). The chips are status, not links; the tabs take you to a page.

The sample competition in this guide

The screenshots use a fictional league, the Elite Netball League, with eight clubs named after animals (Falcons, Panthers, Stingrays, Cobras, Grizzlies, Hawks, Rhinos and Wolves) across Premier, Men's and Under 21 divisions, playing at the Metro Arena. Its sponsors are fictional too. Everything you see is what Studio draws for any competition once its setup is in.

Getting started

Install the app, import or make your competition, and learn the one rule that matters on stream night: keep a Studio window open.

Install

  1. Download FirstPass Studio.dmg, drag the app to Applications and open it. It runs on Apple silicon Macs (M1 and later).
  2. The 1P icon appears in the menu bar, off air. Choose Open Studio. A short splash shows, Studio goes on air and the Output page opens in the app's own window.
  3. Go to Competitions setup and either Import a template file (your league's .json, with its logos and sponsors inside) or New competition and fill it in (Chapter 6).
  4. Back on Output, add the graphics you need with Add page, and turn on the video output that feeds your switcher (Chapter 3).

On air and off air

The pages zoom like Safari's, for a small screen: Zoom In (⌘+), Zoom Out (⌘−) and Actual Size (⌘⌥0) in the View menu, which shows the level, or a pinch on the trackpad. Every Studio window zooms together and the level is kept on that Mac; while it isn't 100% a chip at the right of the bar shows it, and a click on the chip puts it back. The Docs tab, this guide, has its contents at the left on a wide window.

Studio only runs while a Studio window is open. Opening one (Open Studio, any page in the 1P menu, or ⌘N) puts it on air; closing the last one takes it off air, and the graphics and Stream Deck get no answer until a window opens again. Closing the last window while on air asks first, and so does ⌘Q, so a stray keypress can't stop a stream. Leave the Output page open all night.

The bar's chips show the game on the board, whether PlayHQ is answering (amber when overdue, red after a minute of errors) and which outputs are on air, on every page; the Output page's status board shows the score and the rest of the night. If anything is wrong, the page you keep open says so.

The 1P menu

The 1P icon in the menu bar is Studio's home: it opens Studio, its pages and this guide, backs the settings up and restores them, and has Open at login, Appearance (the Studio pages follow the Mac's light or dark look unless pinned here; the graphics never change) and Check for updates. Chapter 10 lists every item. Updates download in the background and install when the app quits, never mid-stream.

Links

Studio's links start with the Mac's Bonjour name, such as http://matts-macbook-pro.local:8080/, so the same link works from a phone, another computer or a Stream Deck on the same network. http://localhost:8080 works on the Mac itself.

Before you go on air

  1. Open the app. The Output page opens and Studio goes on air. Keep a window open all night.
  2. Right competition. Competitions setup shows the one in use at the top. Check its PlayHQ link and the scoring window, and that the Production Planner has tonight's court.
  3. Right game. On Scoreboard control, the picked game is tonight's first on your court and Follow PlayHQ is on.
  4. Scores arrive. Once the scorer has started on PlayHQ, the control page says the game is being live scored and the board moves. Nothing is read before the scoring window opens, so a quiet board before then is normal.
  5. Scene and output. On Output, turn on the display, NDI or Blackmagic output your switcher takes, check fill and key or chroma, and show one graphic to see it land.
  6. Sound. Play a few seconds of music and an animation with sound, and check the sound only comes out where you want it.
  7. Stream Deck. Press a Source key and watch the Sources list follow it. A score key switches scoring to Manual, so put it back to Follow PlayHQ after testing.
  8. Sponsors. Tonight's spots and ads are on; the sponsor report starts counting when the output turns on.

How it fits together

Four things do the work, and each one is set up once:

  • Follow PlayHQ. The scoreboard reads the live score, quarter and clock from the same feed the PlayHQ game page uses, every 5 seconds by default (as often as every second), while someone live-scores the game on PlayHQ. Automatic game selection moves the board to each next game on your court as its electronic scoring starts.
  • The base scene. A saved scene with the sources that stay on through the game: the scoreboard, the title banner, the branding logos and the score ticker.
  • Automatic layers. Sources that stay on but only show when something happens: the super shot celebration, the hot streaks and game moments, the super shot tab on the scoreboard, the naming-rights sponsor's tab. They watch the board and play by themselves.
  • Sequences. Steps played in order for the parts of the night that aren't live play: the run-up, the walk out, quarter and half time, full time and the close. A sequence can start by itself from the game's clock, carry music, and hand over to another sequence.

Scoreboard control

The page is laid out for the person scoring. The bar at the top names the game on the board with its division, round, court and time, holds the Follow PlayHQ / Manual switch, and says which game the board will switch to next; Change game… opens the Game section at the bottom, where the game night, court, automatic selection and PlayHQ settings live (it folds to its title once the night is set). Under the bar are the two scores with their +1 and +2 pads, the quarter buttons and the clock with Start / Stop, and the keys printed on each button. Beneath them, tabs for the game graphics (each opens on its own, to check it), the team lists, the finals and the rest, with a preview of the board beside them.

Always a game on

When Studio starts, it puts a game on the board with Follow PlayHQ on: the game on now on your court, otherwise the first game of tonight on that court. Games are picked by game night, not by round: the list holds every division's games that date, whatever round each is up to, so one division on Round 10 and another on Finals Round 1 sit side by side, each game showing its own round. Tonight is found by date (the first game night in the next 5 days, counting today), so a Tuesday night is found from the Friday before. You can pick another night from the Game night list, which names each night's rounds; its games on your court are then followed by their time (the one due now, otherwise the next to come). Set it back to Automatic afterwards.

Automatic game selection

With Pick the game automatically for court ticked, the board shows the first game of the night on that court, then switches to each next game when its electronic scoring starts (Electronic scoring starts N minutes before the game on the setup page, 10 by default, 15 in the sample league), with the quarter back to Q1 and a full clock. In finals it keeps the final on until PlayHQ marks it finished, in case of extra time. Picking a game from the list by hand turns automatic selection off; tick it again to go back. The court comes from the Production Planner: when a game night arrives, its planned court is set here with from the Production Planner beside it, and automatic selection goes back on. A night with no plan uses the Fallback court to follow from Competitions setup (fallback from Competitions setup). Changing the court here, or picking a game by hand, lasts for that night only (changed by hand for tonight), even if Studio restarts; the Planner flags it so the commentators can be moved, and the next night goes back to the plan.

Follow PlayHQ

With Follow PlayHQ on, the score and quarter update every 5 seconds from PlayHQ's live scoring, and Follow the PlayHQ scorer's clock runs the clock from the scorer's clock. The clock matches the scores: switching to Manual, or pressing any score button, turns clock-following off as well, and the control page says so. Press a clock button to run the clock by hand while scores still follow PlayHQ. To line the clock up with the venue, type the venue's time in Match venue clock.

Nothing before the window

Studio doesn't ask PlayHQ's live feed for a game until its electronic scoring window opens, whether the game was picked automatically or by hand. The control page says when it will start checking.

The Output page

The Output page builds Studio's own scene: sources drawn as layers on one 1920 × 1080 frame, top layer first, and sends it to a display, a Blackmagic device or NDI for your switcher.

It is one page, laid out for the night. The picked output's picture sits at the top with its tabs directly above it, HD or 4K, Settings and Output on in a row over them, and a status board beside it with the score and a line each for the sequence playing, the next one armed (counting down, in days when it's days away), the music, the mics (with an amber line while they duck), the switcher, the recording (with Record or Stop) and, across the board, the next game: its teams, when it starts and when the scoreboard switches to it. Under them the Sources run down the left, with the scenes and sequences as tiles, then music and mics, the switcher and the media folder to their right. Every setting is a click away in its section: a source's settings open under its row, Edit scenes opens the scene grid, Settings on Music and Mics unfolds theirs, and Set up on the switcher its address and keys. A name that's too long for its space ends in …; hover over it to read it in full.

Every section folds away with the arrow beside its title (or a click on the title), so the ones you don't use, say Music or Switcher, take one line. Studio remembers which are folded on that Mac, even after it restarts, and buttons in a folded section's title row, like Output on, still work. The page carries no explanations; this guide has them, and More in the guide under each page's title opens its part.

Sources

Add page adds any Studio graphic (or any web address), Add image a PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP or SVG, and Add video an MP4, WebM or MOV. A new source goes in hidden with its settings open under its row, ready to change. Each row has its On / Off, an eye to preview it on its own and a pencil for its settings. The list is top layer first: drag a source up or down to move it between layers, or use Move up and Move down in its settings. Add all unused pages puts every Studio graphic in the scene at once, hidden.

Source settings

Click a source, or its pencil, and its settings open under its row; Done closes them. A graphic's link options (position, heading, division, round, animate away and so on) show as fields under its address, so there is nothing to look up. On / Off fades it in or out over its own Fade time (0 is a cut). Position and scale place it on the frame. Load the page fresh each time it's shown (on by default) reloads a page as it fades in so its animation replays; untick it to keep a page running so it shows instantly. Preview (and the eye in its row) opens the source on its own, half size, on a checkerboard.

Videos play from the beginning each time they're shown, can loop, and fade out when they finish unless looping; a video that fades out switches its source off, so its key goes dark and the next On plays it again. A ProRes 4444 .mov is converted to a transparent WebM when it is added, keeping its alpha. Export sources saves the whole scene with its media as one zip to keep or move to another Mac; Import sources puts one back.

The base scene

The base scene is what viewers see for most of the game. In the sample league it is the Live game scene: the scoreboard at the bottom, the title banner at the top with the PIVT and FirstPass Studio logos on the left and the competition logo on the right, and the score ticker along the bottom edge with the other courts' scores.

  • Scoreboard. Court in a pink box on the left, both teams in their club colours, the score at screen centre, quarter and clock on the right. "PlayHQ live" shows while scores follow PlayHQ; a "Presented by" tab carries the naming-rights sponsor.
  • Title. Round, competition and venue, with the naming-rights sponsor's pill under it.
  • Branding logos. PIVT and FirstPass Studio left of the title, the competition logo right of it, so other graphics can be turned on and off around them.
  • Score ticker. Scrolling scores from tonight's other games, led by a Partner-and-up sponsor's tagline. Its speed is set on the control page or from the Stream Deck.

Build it once: turn those sources on, then press Save what's on now under Scenes and name it. From then on, one click (or one Stream Deck key) brings the show back to this look.

Automatic layers

These sources are left on in the base scene and play by themselves from the scoreboard. They add no PlayHQ requests of their own; they ask Studio, which already has the score.

Each celebration can carry a sponsor's "Brought to you by" tag (Chapter 7). A division without super shots plays no super shot animation and counts no super goals; that is set on the competition setup page, per division if needed.

Scenes

A scene is a saved set of sources on and off. Clicking a scene goes to it, turning sources on and off to match while anything already on stays on, not reloaded. Scenes are listed by colour in the order of a stream: green pre-game, blue live, purple quarter time, grey post-game, yellow ads.

Turn a source on or off by hand after going to a scene and the scene is marked changed; the bar under the scenes offers Back to saved, Save over it, or Save as new scene. Edit scenes shows a grid, sources down and scenes across, to set each one On, Off or Leave, rename, colour, copy or delete scenes without changing what's on. A source added later is off in every scene.

Quiet scenes

Tick Quiet the music when this starts on a scene for an interview on court: whatever music is playing ducks and keeps playing until the next scene or sequence without the tick.

Sequences

A sequence plays the scene's sources one after another. Each step turns on the sources you pick and stays for a set time, until it finishes (its videos end or its animation animates away), until the game moves on (the clock starts, or the next break or full time), or until you press Next. As a step ends, sources the next step doesn't use go off with their own fades.

Three kinds of step

  • Sources (+ Add sources): one or more sources on together, for a length.
  • A scene (+ Add scene): a starting point or a reset point. What the scene turns on stays on under the next steps and after the sequence. Start a break sequence with the Live game scene and the scoreboard stays up under the stats.
  • Another sequence (+ Add sequence): its steps play in place. Build a mini sequence once (the sample league's Break stats and Sponsor break) and use it inside Quarter time, Half time and Full time. A sequence made only of sequences is marked Master. Tick Hide on a mini sequence to keep it off the list; it still plays inside the others and from the Stream Deck.

+ Add sponsor ads adds a step that plays the next one to five sponsor ads in rotation (Chapter 7).

The run order

Above Scenes and Sequences sits the Run order: tonight's plan as a numbered list of sequences and scenes, in the order they play. Add… puts a scene or sequence at the end (Run-up, Walk out, Live, Quarter time, Live, Half time…), rows drag or move with their arrows, and × takes one out. Play next plays the head now, a sequence or a scene, and Skip drops it without playing it (stepping through a sequence is the Sequences panel's own Next). A sequence that ends looks at the run order first and plays what is queued after it; its own Then runs only when nothing is queued, so Then is the fallback and a sequence built for one night still works alone on another. Whatever is at the head leaves the list as it starts, however it was started. A sequence that starts by itself from the game is put at the head with its few seconds' countdown rather than fired blind: Cancel takes it out again and Play next plays it when the ball is dead. Playing a scene or sequence by hand leaves the list alone unless it was the head, and nothing in the list starts by itself. The status board's Next line and the chip in the bar say what Play next will do. Each output has a run order of its own (pick its tab). A sequence that plays on more than one output says so on its row, "also on Commentary · then Commentary loop": Play next starts it on all of them, as its card does, and after it each output follows its own run order, or its own Then when that's empty. When the run order moves on by itself, another output joins the next sequence only if nothing is playing there; one that was playing the sequence that just ended does its own Then.

Saved… keeps tonight's order under a name for next week, loads one in place of what is queued, deletes one or clears the list. The run order and the saved orders live with the competition's sequences, so a relaunch picks the night up where it was. On the Stream Deck, the plugin's Run order key plays what's next, skips it, loads a saved order or clears the list, and names what plays next in that scene's or sequence's colour so you can see what a press will do; without the plugin, /deck/runorder/next plays what's next and /deck/runorder/skip drops it. The Sequence key's own Next still ends the step on screen.

Music, playlists and mics

The Music player plays songs from the media folder (MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV or AIFF; use music you're licensed to stream), one song or a playlist, shuffled or not. Add a song with Even out its loudness ticked and it is levelled to match the others. Music plays wherever the video sound plays: through the output, or in the Scene View while Video output is off, never twice.

  • Play fades in; Pause and Stop fade out (slower by default). Duck lowers it and keeps it running; Mute keeps it running silently.
  • While a video with its sound on plays, the music fades out and pauses, then fades back in from where it was (or lowers, or carries on: a setting). A themed intro or outro does the same.
  • A sequence can bring its own music, and a sequence inside it plays its own while it runs. Going to a scene fades a sequence's music out.
  • The super shot song cuts in when the super shot period starts in a super shot division, loops, and fades out when the period ends.

Mics. Wireless effects mics on court and crowd mics go out with the music: plug their receivers into the Mac (directly or through an audio interface) and add each one under Mics, with its input and channel (two receivers on one stereo input are two mics, left and right). Each mic's Goes to picks the outputs it's mixed into (Output 1 to start), heard while they're on, never in the Scene View, so a speaker near a mic can't feed back. The first time, the Mac asks whether FirstPass Studio may use the microphone.

  • A mic can be plugged into the switcher instead (an ATEM Mini's Mic 1 or Mic 2): the switcher mixes it and Studio moves its fader for the same level, mutes and ducks. Move the fader on the switcher and that becomes the mic's level; Studio never moves it back.
  • Each mic has a level, a mute, a Low cut for wind and rumble and a meter; Level and Mute all cover every mic, and a limiter stops a shout clipping the stream.
  • The mics duck while music plays, while a video with its sound plays and during a scene that quiets the music (each a tick box), and by hand with Duck. A mic marked Has an external mixer never ducks: the mixer does the fading.
  • Stream Deck: /deck/mics/mute/toggle, /deck/mics/duck/toggle, /deck/mics/up, /deck/mics/down, and one mic with /deck/mic/court-mic/mute/toggle.

HD or 4K

The HD and 4K buttons over the picture set the picture for every output at once. In 4K each NDI and Blackmagic output sends 2160p at the frame rate you picked for it (2160p50 for 1080p50). Nothing else changes: scenes, sources, Stream Deck keys, links, NDI names and the switcher key stay as they are, and pressing HD puts everything back exactly as it was.

  • Sharp where Studio draws. The graphics keep their 1920 × 1080 layout and are drawn at twice the density, so text, shapes, logos drawn by Studio and the animations' sparks and confetti are sharp. Pictures and videos are only as sharp as their files: a 1080p video or a small logo looks softer.
  • Only for 4K gear. An HD switcher can't take 2160p. A Blackmagic device without the 2160p format (an HD UltraStudio) stays at 1080p and its card says so; a display is left alone, since a 4K screen set to look like 1920 × 1080 already draws at 4K.
  • Pick a 4K format directly. In 4K a Blackmagic output's video format list also has the device's own 4K formats, such as 2160p50. In HD they're hidden, and the output sends the 1080p format at the same rate.
  • Any graphic's link works at 4K too. Opened in a browser source or window set to 3840 × 2160, a graphic draws itself at twice the size, with nothing added to its link.
  • Check the Mac keeps up. A 4K output is about four times the work of an HD one; the performance line under the output says whether it's keeping up.

More than one output

One output is all most nights need. To give a camera its own graphics, press Add an output beside Output on (up to four). Each output is its own picture with its own route and its own on/off, so a second output can feed an upstream keyer tied to a camera and its graphics cut with that camera, while Output 1 stays the downstream key over everything. Name an output in its card ("Wide camera", say) and that name is used everywhere, including its Stream Deck links.

  • Sources are shared, on/off is per output. Each source in the list has a coloured pill per output (orange, pink, blue, green for Outputs 1 to 4). Press a pill to show or hide that source on that output only. A new output starts with everything off. The Show filter above the list narrows it to one output.
  • Every output plays its own sound. Each plays the sound of the videos and animations on it. The music plays on the outputs ticked in the music player's Music plays on (Output 1 to start), and a sequence's music on every output playing the sequence. Each mic's Goes to picks its outputs, so a commentators' feed can have the effects mics without the commentators' own voices.
  • Scenes and sequences are shared; each output plays its own. Pick an output's tab over the Scene View and clicking a scene or sequence plays it on that output (the sections say which). Hide all clears that output only (or the one the Sources list's Show is set to), and says which, so clearing a monitor never blanks the stream. Each output has its own current scene and plays one sequence at a time, so the same scene or sequence can be on two outputs at once; coloured dots on scene cards, and on sequence cards each output's tick with how the sequence is there (Playing, Paused, Starting or Stopped (it ended there, played out or cut short); a hollow dot and "off" while that output isn't sending), show where each one is on. Ads there show whose turn it is without using it up, so the order on air stays fair.
  • The Scene View has a tab per output, coloured to match, so you can check what each one is sending.
  • Routes can't be shared. Two outputs can't use the same display, the Blackmagic device or the same NDI name; the earlier output keeps it and the later one says so on its card. Two HDMI displays give either two chroma outputs or one fill and key output. Several NDI outputs can run at once, each with its own name, which suits feeding a switcher's inputs over the network.
  • Stream Deck. /deck/output/2/toggle (or the output's name) turns Output 2 on or off, /deck/output/all/off turns them all off, and /deck/output/2/layer/<source>/toggle shows or hides a source on Output 2. Source settings lists these links for each output.

The Production Planner

The Production Planner tab plans the season ahead: which court Studio streams each game night, and who is on the mic for each game. Studio streams one court a night; a second court is a second Mac running its own Studio, with its own plan.

Commentators

Add the people who call your games to the list on the left. Tick Guest for a special guest. The list belongs to the competition; as you type a name, people from your other competitions are suggested, so someone who calls in two leagues is added in a tap. Click the circle beside a name, or drop a photo on the row, to add their headshot for the Socials tab's Commentary team picture; the small × on it takes it off again.

A night

Running it by hand

When nobody is live-scoring the game on PlayHQ, or PlayHQ stops answering mid-game, every automatic part of Chapter 3 has a button. The board, the breaks and the celebrations can all be driven from Scoreboard control and a Stream Deck.

Studio still needs the competition on PlayHQ for its fixture, ladder, squads and results; what changes in manual mode is who runs the score and the clock. The same competition can be followed one week and run by hand the next, and nothing on the Output page changes between the two.

Two people, not one

A followed game can be run by one person because the score, the clock and the breaks take care of themselves. A game run by hand is a different job: someone has to press a key for every goal within a second or two of it, start and stop the clock with the umpire's whistle, and still call the breaks and the graphics. We recommend two people share it:

Camera operator

  • Frames and follows play on the cameras, and cuts between them on the switcher.
  • Runs the game clock from the whistle, with Start / Stop and Reset clock keys beside the camera controls.
  • Scores the goals as they go in, +1 or +2, because they are already watching the ring.
  • Nothing else: no graphics, no breaks, no music.

Production manager

  • Keeps the Output page open and watches the stream itself, not the court.
  • Corrects the score (−1, −2) and the quarter, and sets the clock when it drifts from the venue's.
  • Plays the run-up, break and full time sequences from their keys, and the lower thirds, ladders and stats during play.
  • Runs the music, the sponsor ads and the end of night, and picks the finals winners.

If one person must do it alone, keep the Stream Deck to the score and clock keys and let the sequences do the breaks, exactly as they do in automated mode: the game triggers follow the scoreboard's clock whoever runs it.

Switching to manual

Press Manual on Scoreboard control, or just press any score key: either switches the board to manual scoring and stops following the PlayHQ clock as well. Press Follow PlayHQ to go back; the score catches up with PlayHQ on the next check and the clock follows the scorer's again.

When PlayHQ drops out

If PlayHQ stops answering during a followed game, the scoreboard keeps its last score, the ladder, fixtures and game list keep what they last read, and the debug log records each failed read. Switch to Manual and run the board by hand; Follow PlayHQ picks it up again when PlayHQ is back, and corrects the score to the scorer's.

The score

Each team has +1, +2, −1 and −2. A +2 is a super shot: it scores two and plays the super shot celebration, so a +2 pressed by mistake is fixed with −2 (which plays nothing). Reset scores to 0 clears both. The hot streaks and game moments still play in manual mode, because Studio works out the goal order from the presses just as it does from PlayHQ's score.

KeyWhat it does
Home +1 / Away +1A goal.
Home +2 / Away +2A super shot: two points and the celebration.
−1, −2Take a goal or a super shot back, quietly.
Reset scores to 0Both scores to zero, for the next game.
Super shot (home / away)The celebration only, score unchanged: for a test, or a super shot that was scored as +1 +1.

The clock and the quarter

Start and Stop are one key on the Stream Deck. Reset clock puts a full quarter back (the competition's quarter length; a key can set any time). +10 s and −10 s nudge it, and a key can add or take off any amount. Type the venue's clock in Match venue clock to line the two up, and untick Show clock for a game with no reliable clock at all. Quarters are Q1 to Q4 plus extra time, or H1 and H2 for a competition played in halves; Next quarter steps through them. Changing the quarter doesn't touch the clock, so make the quarter key a Stream Deck Multi Action that also resets the clock.

Breaks and celebrations by hand

A sequence that starts by itself at quarter time is started by its key too, so the production manager can run Quarter time the moment the siren goes instead of waiting for the clock, or run Half time early when a game is called. Next, Pause and Stop work on whatever is playing, and Automatic sequences on on the Output page can be unticked for a night where every break is called by hand.

  • Winner. One key. The celebration reads the board's score when it comes on, so it covers a home win, an away win and a draw. Check the score first. A source with Result set to home, away or draw forces it.
  • Super shot and streaks, once. Besides the automatic sources, a Super shot source set to Plays: home or away, or a Hot streaks source set to one streak, plays once each time it is turned on. Don't keep a manual source and the automatic one on together, or it plays twice.
  • The Clash. A pre-game animation, played from its key or as a step in the walk-out sequence, with the two teams, their logos and club colours.
  • Lower thirds. The person lower third's name box on the Output page suggests the competition's athletes; type a guest's name and title for a commentator or an official. Turn it on when they appear and off a few seconds later.
  • Stats during play. The ladder lower third, shooting stats, last 10 goals and match up are all Source keys. Each loads fresh when shown, so it has the latest numbers.

Finals

PlayHQ often has no finals fixtures until the day. In Finals on Scoreboard control, pick the division, then pick each game's winner (the score is optional) as the finals are played; the finals graphic and the ladder's finals key move on with each pick. A result from PlayHQ, when it has one, always wins over one picked here.

Competitions setup

Everything about a competition that doesn't come from PlayHQ lives on one page: its PlayHQ link, the names on the graphics, the game rules, finals, game day, colours, font and logos. Set it up once a season and every graphic follows.

Studio keeps a setup per competition. The one marked In use is what every graphic, Scoreboard control, the Sponsors page and the Stream Deck use. Changes aren't kept until you press Save (or Save and use this competition), and leaving the page with unsaved changes asks first.

PlayHQ

Paste the address of the organisation's page on playhq.com and press Find its competitions; then pick the competition and the season. For a carnival or a league run under another body, use the organisation the competition is listed under. Nothing else about PlayHQ is needed: no account, no key.

Names on the graphics

The competition name on the title banner, the organisation name as the ladder's heading, and the venue for the title, fixture and Up next. PlayHQ's names are often long or formal; these are the ones viewers see.

Game rules

Quarters or halves and their length set the clock, the quarter buttons and the wording of the break cards. Super shots turns on the +2 scoring, the celebration, the super shot tab in the last minutes of each quarter and the Super columns on the shooting graphics; a division can have its own setting, by its name or by a word in it ("Open", "Men"). The break lengths let the sequence editor say how many loops of a break sequence fit in the break.

Finals

Pick the finals format for the competition, or per division: it decides the finals places the ladder marks (double chance or a week off, elimination, playing for third) and the bracket the finals graphic draws week by week. PlayHQ's finals games are used when it has them; otherwise the winners are picked on Scoreboard control. The winners are called picks Champions or Premiers for the Socials tab's picture of the grand final winners.

Game night

The Fallback court to follow, when electronic scoring starts before each game (10 minutes by default; 20 or 30 when games are further apart) and how many days ahead tonight's games are found. The Production Planner sets each game night's court; the fallback is only used on a night with no plan, which is mostly before the Planner is first used, since a night you haven't planned takes the night before's plan. Every game night starts with the game picked automatically. Scoreboard control can change the court, pick a game by hand or pick another game night, for that night only. On a carnival day with several rounds, this round moves on as each round's first game comes up. Running top shooters are called sets the Socials picture's name: Automatic calls it Tournament top shooters when the fixture runs under 15 days and Season top shooters otherwise.

Colours and font

The competition's colours, see-through dark panels, and the font. Paste a brand kit's text into Brand kit colours and every HEX or RGB value in it becomes a swatch in each colour picker. Add a font file (TTF, OTF, WOFF or WOFF2, one per weight or a variable font) and it travels with the competition and its template, so the graphics look the same on any Mac; every font is sized to match the capitals of Montserrat, which the graphics were drawn with. The preview under the pickers shows a rough title and scoreboard in the colours before you save.

Logos

The competition logo has a light mode and a dark mode version (one is used for both if that's all you add); it shows beside the title in the branding logos, above the title with its option on, in the corner of the ladder and in the logo animations. Team logos are matched to PlayHQ team names by file name, so drop in a folder of files named after the teams. Logos and colours belong to clubs: every team of a club, in every division, gets the club's logo and colour, worked out from the team name without its division ("Falcons 21U" is Falcons). Fix a wrong match in the Club box. A club colour goes behind its teams' names on the scoreboard and behind their logo on The Clash; without one the competition's colours are used.

Live viewers

Add the YouTube channels and Facebook Pages the competition streams to. While a video output is on, Studio adds up how many are watching every 30 seconds, for the Output page, the viewers graphic (which shows only from the minimum set here) and the viewer-minutes in the sponsor report. The page explains how to get a YouTube API key and a Facebook Page token; both are kept on this Mac only, never in iCloud, templates or exports.

Templates, export and more

Export as template saves the competition's settings, team logos, competition logo, font and sponsors (with their logos) as one .json file to import on any Studio or keep as a backup; Import a template file adds it. A PlayHQ Client ID is only needed if PlayHQ's game page has no line-ups and the association has given you one. Delete this competition removes it; deleting the one in use switches the graphics to another.

Tiers

Five fixed ranks, named per competition: the Names preset (Naming rights, Elite, Major, Partner, Supporter), the Metals preset (Naming rights, Platinum, Gold, Silver, Bronze) or your own. Each tier's share of screen time decides how turns are split when a sponsor has no target of its own. Each tier gets different places:

TierWhere it shows
Naming rightsA tab on the scoreboard, under the title banner, first on the sponsor wall, and everything below.
EliteThe sponsored moments (super shot, streaks), ads in the breaks, the break card, and everything below.
MajorAds and break cards, "presented by" footers on the stats cards, countdown and top shooters.
PartnerA slot in the score ticker and the logo spots.

The vacancy card has a heading, a contact, a message and a link or email for its QR code, so an empty tier finds its own sponsor.

A sponsor

A sponsor has a name, tagline, website, offer, brand colour, category, deal dates, and optional targets per stream (minutes on screen, ad plays). Category keeps competing sponsors apart in rotation. Add a colour logo and a white one for dark panels (without a white one, the colour logo is made white). A sponsor is kept once and can be shown on several competitions at its own tier in each; sponsors whose deal has ended stay greyed and the graphics skip them.

Ads are videos or stills (up to 10 per sponsor) in the media folder. A Sponsor ads step in a sequence plays the next ads due, taking turns fairly by tier, each video to its end with sound and each still for its seconds; tick House ad and the PIVT and FirstPass Studio ad plays when nothing is due. The QR code points to the sponsor's website with tracking tags, so visits from the stream show in their analytics; save it as a PNG for the sponsor's own use.

Spots and moments

Every spot picks its sponsor from the Sponsors page by default. Each graphic's Sponsor option can pin one sponsor or turn the spot off. Sponsored moments sells the super shot, each hot streak and each game moment to one sponsor for the season, whose name appears in a "brought to you by" tag on the animation; by default the Elite sponsors take turns.

Proof of play

While a video output is on, Studio logs every second each sponsor is on screen and every ad played, matched to the scene's sources, and reads the live viewer count every 30 seconds. Sponsor report turns that into a page per night (or a run of nights) with minutes on screen, ad plays, viewer-minutes, peak and average viewers per sponsor, ready to print or save as a PDF for the sponsor. A tick box counts time with no video output on, for a rehearsal. With more than one output, only Output 1 and outputs with Counts for sponsors ticked are counted, and each counts as on air only while it's on: an output set to Web links only is off.

Photos

Data and bandwidth

The graphics only ever talk to Studio on the Mac. Studio alone contacts PlayHQ: one small request every 5 seconds for the followed game (or as set, down to one a second), the ticker's other courts every 30 seconds, the fixture about once a minute for automatic selection, and the rest once when a graphic loads. The Production Planner and the Socials tab read the same fixture, results and ladders through the same cache, and finished games' line ups once, so they add nothing; the Fixture board reads other courts' live scores the ticker's way, so it adds nothing while the ticker is on and at most one request a minute per live game when it isn't. Commentator stats' auto-refresh asks Studio only; its team lists are read at the start and at each quarter break. Finished games are read once and kept. Extra outputs read the same data from Studio, so they add nothing here. It all comes to well under a megabyte an hour, so a phone hotspot is enough for Studio; the stream itself is the bandwidth.

The Mac app

FirstPass Studio needs an Apple silicon Mac (M1 or later), and fits a 13" MacBook's screen. A plain M1 drives one extra display; Pro and Max chips drive more, which fill and key over two displays needs. A Blackmagic output needs Blackmagic Desktop Video, and NDI needs NDI's free runtime (both under The Output page in detail); the app brings everything else with it, ffmpeg for video conversion included.

The app always starts in the menu bar off air, whether opened at login (tick Open at login) or by hand, so nothing talks to PlayHQ until you open Studio. On air it serves the graphics and the Stream Deck links, talks to PlayHQ, and restarts by itself if it stops. Stop Studio in the 1P menu closes the Studio windows and goes off air.

The app window is like any Mac app's: no address bar, tabs or bookmarks, the Studio header is the title bar (drag it to move the window), and it shows in the Dock and ⌘Tab while a window is open. Links that open a new tab on Studio (a preview, the control or setup page) open another window; links elsewhere (PlayHQ, GitHub) open in your browser. The menu at the top of the screen has File > Open in Browser (⇧⌘O) and Copy Page Link (⇧⌘C) for the page in front, Go for each Studio page (⌘1 to ⌘9, and ⌘0 for Docs), View for reload, back and page zoom (in steps, the level shown in the menu; Zoom In ⌘+, Zoom Out ⌘−, Actual Size ⌘⌥0, or pinch on the trackpad), Help > FirstPass Studio User Guide for this guide, New Window (⌘N), and Close Studio Windows (⌥⌘W) in the FirstPass Studio menu.

Links and access

Studio's links (on the Devices tab, Scoreboard control and in the Mac app's menu, Copy the Studio link too) start with this Mac's Bonjour name, such as http://studio-mac.local:8080, instead of localhost. It is the Local hostname in System Settings > General > Sharing, so the same link works on this Mac and from a phone, another computer or a Stream Deck on the same network. The port stays 8080, and http://localhost:8080 still works on this Mac, so links and Stream Deck buttons you already have carry on. To change it, set "linkHost" in config.json in the data folder: "bonjour" (the default), "localhost", or any name or IP address, then choose Restart Studio in the 1P menu.

Only this Mac can change things in the menu bar icon sets remoteChanges, and is ticked again every time the app opens: ticked, phones, other computers and Stream Decks on the network that aren't in Devices can still open every graphic but can't change scores, the scene, sequences, settings or media, or read private data (the settings and scene backups, the athlete register, the debug log, the sponsor reports, the competition setup); Studio answers those with a 403 that says so. Either way a change must come from Studio's own pages or a program (the Stream Deck plugin, a Website key): a request a browser marks as coming from a web page on another site, or that asks for Studio by a name this computer doesn't answer to, gets a 403 too, and so does a read of private data asked for by such a name. A Stream Deck link must carry this Mac's key (/k/<key>/deck/…, as Studio copies them; kept in devices.json, never backed up) or be signed by a paired Studio plugin, or come from Studio's own pages, so a web page can't press one. A phone, iPad or another computer pairs from /pair.html: the Mac offers Allow scoring only (the scores, clock, quarter and game) or Allow everything, and the device then works while the box is ticked; Devices can change its level or remove it. (Until a plugin has paired, the plugin's older x-studio-deck header still counts, so plugins installed before pairing keep working until a new one is allowed.)

On a network you don't control, leave Only this Mac can change things ticked, or block incoming connections to FirstPass Studio in the Mac's firewall.

The data folder

Everything Studio saves lives in one data folder on this computer, outside the app, so updates never touch it: ~/Library/Application Support/FirstPass Studio (the setup page shows where, and Open the settings folder in the 1P menu opens it).

  • competitions/: a folder per competition with its setup (settings.json), team logos, competition logo, athlete photos and athlete register, its sequences, run orders and the Production Planner's nights (sequences.json, planner.json), and active.json, the one in use.
  • sponsors/: every sponsor once (sponsors.json), a folder of logos each, and exposure/, the log the sponsor report is made from.
  • scene.json, outputs.json, music.json and mics.json: the sources and saved scenes, the video outputs, the music player and playlists, and the mics, shared by every competition; media/: the images, videos and songs they use.
  • control.json: Scoreboard control's choices (the game night picked by hand, automatic game selection and its court, following the PlayHQ clock, how often to check PlayHQ, showing the clock, the ticker speed, finals winners picked by hand).
  • scoreboard-state.json: the game on the board, the scores and the clock.
  • config.json (optional): changes this computer's settings (port, log size, how often to check PlayHQ, linkHost, remoteChanges) from the app's defaults (see Links and access).
  • devices.json: the Stream Deck key, link sets and the devices allowed to change things, on this Mac only and never backed up.
  • switcher.json: the ATEM switcher Studio controls (its name on the network, or the address typed) and the stinger's line-up in frames, one for the Mac whichever competition is in use.
  • app-settings.json: the Mac app's own choices (where the window was).
  • recordings.json, demos.json (saved demo games), replay.json (what a 4K replay follows, and each recording's sync) and Recordings/ (on this Mac, not in iCloud Drive or a backup): whether Studio records each PlayHQ game on air and for how long after full time, and the recordings themselves.
  • logs/: the debug log. backups/: a copy of the folder made before an update changes how settings are saved.

Settings in iCloud Drive

In the 1P menu, Settings folder > Move to iCloud Drive… asks for a folder in iCloud Drive (make one called FirstPass Studio, say) and moves the settings into a subfolder named after this Mac, its Local hostname in System Settings > General > Sharing: iCloud Drive/FirstPass Studio/Studio-MacBook-Pro. Each Mac signed in to the same Apple account gets its own subfolder, so two Studios never save over each other's settings, and a second Mac with the same name is stopped from using the first one's. Studio goes off air for the move and comes straight back. Before anything moves, a copy of the settings is saved in backups/ in the folder on this Mac, and the originals are removed only once every file has copied across. If the folder already has settings for this Mac (from before), Studio asks whether to use those or replace them.

Some files always stay on this Mac: the debug log, the scores and clock (scoreboard-state.json, saved every few seconds during a game), PlayHQ caches, the app's window positions and data-location.json, which says where the settings are. If iCloud has kept only a placeholder of a file (Optimise Mac Storage), Studio downloads it before going on air; to keep it all on the Mac, right-click the folder in Finder and choose Keep Downloaded. If the iCloud folder can't be found (iCloud Drive turned off, signed out), Studio says so and doesn't start, rather than starting with no settings. Settings folder > Move back to this Mac… moves them back.

Settings folder > Restore from another Mac… copies another Mac's settings (competitions, scenes, sequences, music, Scoreboard control's choices) over this Mac's: pick the Mac from the list of folders beside this one, or choose its folder. The other Mac keeps its own; this Mac's settings are backed up in backups/ on this Mac first, and are only replaced once the copy has checked out. If this Mac's settings are on the Mac, they go into iCloud Drive too, in a new folder for this Mac beside the other Mac's, and stay there from then on. Studio goes off air for the copy and comes straight back. Open the settings folder opens wherever they are now.

Backup and restore

Back up… in the 1P menu saves every setting in one zip: competitions with their logos and athletes, sponsors, scenes, sequences, music and playlists, the media folder, Scoreboard control's choices and config.json, wherever they are kept (on the Mac or in iCloud Drive). The debug log, the scores and clock, PlayHQ caches and stream keys stay out. First it asks for notes (what the backup is for, say) and whether to include the media folder (videos, songs), which can make the zip big. The zip is named FirstPassStudioBackup-2026-10-24_10-46-02.zip, the date and time it was made, so each one is new and they sort oldest to newest. Beside it goes a summary with the same name, FirstPassStudioBackup-2026-10-24_10-46-02.txt: the notes, then the names of the competitions (and which is in use), sequences, playlists, sources, scenes and sponsors. Inside the zip is one folder of the same name holding the settings as they are in the settings folder, the same summary.txt, and studio-export.json saying which Mac and Studio version made it. Files iCloud hasn't downloaded yet are downloaded first. Studio stays on air while it backs up, and works off air too. Link for scripts: /api/settings/export downloads the same zip (add ?media=0 to leave media out, &notes=… for notes).

Restore… in the 1P menu puts a backup back: pick a FirstPassStudioBackup-….zip, and Studio shows when and on which Mac it was made, its notes and how many competitions, sequences, playlists, sources, scenes and sponsors it holds, then asks before replacing this Mac's settings with it (on the Mac or in iCloud Drive, wherever they are kept now). This Mac's settings are first copied to backups/ in the folder on this Mac, and only replaced once the whole backup has unpacked and checked out, so a damaged zip changes nothing. Either way this Mac keeps its own Only this Mac can change things, port and link name, and the ffmpeg and PlayHQ addresses in its config.json. A backup made without the media folder keeps this Mac's media. The debug log, scores and clock aren't touched. Studio goes off air while the settings swap and comes straight back. A backup from an older Studio is brought up to date as Studio starts; one from a newer Studio opens with competitions read-only until this Mac's Studio is updated.

Unsaved changes

Changes on the Competitions setup page aren't kept until you press Save. With changes not saved yet, leaving the page (a tab, or the Go menu), opening another competition, Use this one, New competition or Import a template file first asks Save, Don't save or Keep editing. Export as template asks to save first, since the file is made from the saved settings. Closing the window, reloading or quitting asks whether to leave without saving.

The editors that open over a page (a sponsor, a sequence, a playlist, a mic or a scene's Switcher actions on the Output page, an athlete's Games) ask the same question: with changes not saved yet, pressing Escape or leaving the page from the Go menu asks Save, Don't save or Keep editing, and closing the window asks whether to leave without saving. Their Cancel button still just cancels.

Data and bandwidth

scoreboard.html, goals.html and countdown.html (and Scoreboard control) follow the scoreboard over the one connection Studio's pages keep open for the scene, so a score press or a PlayHQ reading reaches them the moment it happens, with no asking in between. ticker.html, viewers.html (every 15 seconds, Studio only) and the automatic supershot.html and streak.html keep asking for new data; ticker.html also asks for its speed every 3 seconds. None of that reaches PlayHQ. commentator.html follows the scoreboard live for its clock, moments and run, and with Auto-refresh (on by default there) asks Studio for its stats every 15 seconds and for who is on the mic each minute, neither of which reaches PlayHQ; its team lists load at the start and each quarter break (see below). The super shot and hot streak pages ask Studio every second for the scoreboard and nothing else, so they add no PlayHQ requests. Every other graphic gets its data once when it loads, and again each time it's shown on the Output page (with Load the page fresh each time it's shown, the default). The Clash and the winner celebration only retry if Studio isn't running.

Pages only ever talk to Studio on this computer, which uses no internet. Studio is the only thing that contacts PlayHQ, and it caches what it gets:

  • Scoreboard following PlayHQ: one small request every 5 seconds by default (Check PlayHQ every on Scoreboard control, 1 to 120 seconds), starting when the game's electronic scoring starts (set on the setup page). Nothing is asked before then. Studio makes this request itself whatever windows are open, so the score, the goal order and the game triggers (quarter breaks, full time) keep up even while the scoreboard is hidden and Scoreboard control is closed; the pages share that reading rather than adding to it.
  • Studio scene (scene.html and the Output page): nothing. They keep one connection open to Studio, which only sends when the scene changes; the pages in the scene ask for what they normally do.
  • Display output (Mac app): nothing. The output windows show the scene page, so they ask for what the scene's pages normally ask for, once per display (fill and key means the pages run twice). The Blackmagic and NDI outputs run them once more, off screen, and each extra output runs the pages turned on for it once more. NDI's video goes over your local network only, never the internet. In 4K nothing more is asked for, but each NDI source carries about four times the pixels (roughly 250 to 400 Mbit/s at 50 or 60 fps, and fill and key is two sources), so use one source with transparency on a gigabit network, and the Mac does about four times the work per 4K output: the Output page's performance line shows whether it keeps up.
  • Mics: nothing over the internet. Each output window mixing mics tells Studio what it hears twice a second, and the Output page asks for it twice a second while its Mics section is open; the sound itself stays inside the Mac. A mic on the switcher is a few small fader messages over the switcher's connection when its level changes (about 20 a second while a dip fades).
  • Switcher (an ATEM, if one is set up): nothing over the internet. Studio keeps one connection to the switcher on your local network, a few kilobytes a second of small packets each way; looking for switchers asks the local network once.
  • User guide (/guide/): nothing over the internet. It comes with Studio, fonts and pictures included, about 4 MB when it opens and nothing after.
  • Stream Deck plugin's keys: nothing. The plugin keeps one connection open to Studio while any of its keys is on screen (and one more while a Switcher key is), and Studio only sends when the scene or the switcher changes.
  • Recordings: nothing over the internet. Studio writes what it already has to a file on this Mac every 5 seconds while a game is recording, a megabyte or so a game. A practice game asks PlayHQ nothing at all: the recording answers.
  • 4K replay following a HyperDeck: nothing over the internet. While a replay follows it, Studio keeps one connection to the HyperDeck on your local network and asks where it is four times a second, a few hundred bytes each time.
  • Countdown: nothing. The clock ticks in the page and the start time comes from the game already picked.
  • Live viewers (Competitions setup): only while a video output is on, one small request per stream every 30 seconds to YouTube or Facebook, plus a YouTube search when a channel's live video isn't known yet (at most every 5 minutes). Nothing when no streams are set up.
  • Last 10 goals: nothing extra. It shares the scoreboard's 5 second reading, and Studio works out the goal order from it.
  • Commentator stats: the stats, shooting % included, share the scoreboard's reading, so auto-refresh adds nothing. Its team lists ask for the game's line up when the page comes on and at each quarter break, about four small requests a game; games played, ladder, form, head to head and up next are read once when the page comes on and for each new game (from the season Studio already keeps); games played reads the division's finished games once (kept in game-cache.json, as for the Athletes page).
  • Ticker: other courts' scores at most every 30 seconds.
  • Automatic game selection: the fixture about once a minute for the current round, every 10 minutes for the rest.
  • Socials tab: nothing extra. Its previews and pictures read the season's fixture, results and ladders through the same cache, and the Production Planner, sponsors and logos from Studio. Top shooters and Tournament top shooters read each finished game's line up once and keep it, the same as the Top shooters graphic. Finals bound and the Finals draw (and a finals night's other pictures) read the division's finals the way the Finals graphic does, through the same cache.
  • Production Planner: nothing extra. It reads the season's fixture through the same cache as automatic game selection (the current round about once a minute, the rest every 10 minutes), and the commentators graphics ask Studio only. The fixture board's live scores for other courts go through the ticker's reader and cache: nothing extra while the ticker is on, at most one request a minute per live game when it isn't.
  • Athletes page: when you open a team, the line up of each of its games this season, a few at a time. Finished games are only ever read once: Studio keeps their line ups in game-cache.json in the data folder, so they're kept through restarts too.
  • Finals: the division's ladder and its season's rounds when the page loads (the rounds are kept for 10 minutes, the current one for a minute).
  • Team lists: warmed up ahead, so they show straight away. When a game is picked (by hand or automatically) and when Studio starts, it reads the game's line up and both teams' season squads, and the next game's teams on the same court too. Finished games come from game-cache.json, so after the first time that's only a few small requests per game picked. Squads are kept for 30 minutes; the team list itself asks for the game's line up and score when it loads.

Each answer is a few kilobytes, well under 1 MB an hour in total. Two options make a page poll, both off unless you add them: ?refresh= on the ladder, and &reload= on the scoreboard, scene and Scoreboard control.

Animations run at the same speed at any frame rate, 50p or 60p.

Sponsors in detail

A sponsor's QR code is made on this Mac, with tracking tags for the sponsor's website analytics unless they're turned off; Save QR code as PNG gives a copy. Pull up and Pull down take a sponsor from the nearest tier below or above that has one. The vacancy card is never shown without a link or email for its QR code. The Sponsor report covers any run of nights: time on screen and where, ad plays, time with their QR code up, and progress against their targets. What's on an output counts as on air only while that output is on: one that's turned off or set to Web links only isn't, even while another is. Print or save as PDF to send it to them.

Sponsors on the graphics: the naming rights sponsor (or what its empty-tier rule gives instead) is a Presented by tab over the home team on the scoreboard, a pill under the title banner and a line under the countdown. The game stats, shooting stats and top shooters cards carry the next sponsor in rotation from Major and up in their footer, the ticker leads with the next from Partner and up, and the end of night card (Up next after the last game) thanks the sponsors from Major and up with their logos (?sponsors=all every tier, ?sponsors=0 none). On any of them, ?sponsor=0 leaves the sponsor off and ?sponsor=Golds%20Bakery always shows that one; set it in the source's options on the Output page. With no sponsors they look as they always did.

Competitions setup in detail

When an update improves one of the ready-made setups (AMMNA Nationals, GIG WANL), yours picks up only what you haven't changed yourself. Section by section:

  • Game rules: quarters or halves and their length, and super shots, for the whole competition or per division. Under the super shots tick box each PlayHQ division can follow the competition, or have super shots or not. A setting can also be saved for a word instead of a whole division name, and then covers every division with that word in its name ("Open" covers "Division 1 Open"; "Men" covers "Men's" but not "Women's"); a division's own setting wins over a word. GIG WANL comes set for super shots in Open only, not 21&Under or Men's, by the words "Open", "21" and "Men". A division without super shots doesn't play the super shot animation for a +2 press or count goals as super shots, and its shooting stats and top shooters leave the Super column off. Super shots in the last [x] minutes of each quarter (default 3) is when the scoreboard shows its "Super shot" tab; 0 leaves it off.
  • Finals: the competition's finals format, and a different one for any division that needs it. Top 2: grand final, or best of three. Top 4: semis then grand final, Page (double chance for 1 and 2), or a placings day (1 v 2 and 3 v 4). Top 6: byes for 1 and 2, or a double chance for 1 and 2. Top 8: AFL style, straight knockout, or carnival placings (a knockout over three weeks with play-offs, so every team gets a place 1 to 8). Every team: everyone plays on grand final day, 1 v 2 for the title, 3 v 4 for third, 5 v 6 for fifth and so on down the ladder, with nobody knocked out (an odd team out has no game). The ladder marks the finals places by what each earns and the finals graphic follows it.
  • Game night: the court the scoreboard follows, whether it picks the game automatically, Electronic scoring starts [x] minutes before the game (default 10; set 20 or 30 when games are further apart), and how many days ahead counts as this game night. Automatic selection switches to the next game when its electronic scoring starts, and the scoreboard doesn't ask PlayHQ for a game's score before then, even for a game picked by hand: Scoreboard control says when it will start checking. That keeps PlayHQ's "game could not be found or was not electronically scored" error out of the debug log.
  • Colours and font: nine colours, how see-through the dark panels are, and the font, with a preview. Click a colour's swatch for a colour picker (shade square, hue slider, HEX and RGB boxes, and on Chrome or Edge Pick from screen), or type or paste a colour into its box as HEX (#7f4092) or RGB (127, 64, 146). Under Brand kit colours, paste any text from a brand kit with HEX or RGB values in it; those colours are saved with the competition and show in every picker, one click to use.
  • Font: the Font box lists the fonts that come with Studio first (they work on any computer, with no internet), then fonts added to the competition, then every font installed on this Mac (including any you add in Font Book), such as Helvetica Neue, Avenir Next, Futura and DIN Condensed. A Mac font shows only on this Mac: a graphic opened on another computer uses Montserrat instead. Add a font file takes TTF, OTF, WOFF or WOFF2 files (one per weight, or one variable font); they're saved with the competition and go in its template file, so it looks the same on any Mac. Add only fonts you're allowed to use, such as open licence fonts from Google Fonts. Any letter a font lacks comes from Montserrat. Every font is sized and centred to match Montserrat, which the graphics were drawn with, so text fills its boxes the same whichever font you pick; narrow fonts stand a little taller so they don't look small. Text size (80 to 130%) makes a font other than Montserrat bigger or smaller on every graphic; keep an eye on long team names.
  • Logos: the competition logo, in a light mode version (for a light picture behind it) and a dark mode version (for a dark one; if you add only one, it is used for both), and the team logos. The logos page and title use the light mode logo unless ?mode=dark is added; the ladder's corner, on its coloured header, uses the dark mode one unless ?mode=light is added. Drop team logo files on the page; they match clubs by file name, and the PlayHQ team list shows which logo each team gets, with a box to pick a different one.
  • Clubs: a team's logo and colour belong to its club, so every team of a club in every division shares them, and changing one row changes them all. The club is the team's name without the division on the end ("Comets 21U", "Comets Men" and "Comets Open" are all Comets; "Stormz Div 2" is Stormz). The Club box in the team list links a team to a different club, or to a new one.
  • Club colours: in the same PlayHQ team list, Add colour gives a club its own colour. The scoreboard puts it behind its teams' names and The Clash behind their logo and name, with dark or white text, whichever reads better. Clubs without one (or after ×) use the competition's colours. The match up page always uses the competition's colours. Pick the colour with the colour picker or paste it as HEX or RGB.

Use this one switches every graphic, Scoreboard control and the Stream Deck to that competition: the game night goes back to automatic and the scoreboard starts on that competition's first game. Saving the competition in use reloads the graphics with the changes. Setups are kept in the competitions folder (see The data folder, above).

Athletes in detail

Teams are listed by division, so a club with a team in more than one division (Knights) has a button in each, showing that division's line ups; All athletes lists everyone once. A silhouette's gender is the one picked on the page if you've picked one, else PlayHQ's gender for the athlete, else the PlayHQ gender of their division when they play in a men's or women's division (mixed and open divisions leave it neutral). Silhouette on each athlete shows where it came from and can override it. Approve none takes photo approval away from every athlete listed. Games played can be kept per club as well as per division.

The Output page in detail

  • Sources: Add page adds any Studio graphic or web address; Add image adds a PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP or SVG, and Add video an MP4, WebM or MOV (saved in the data folder's media folder). Each opens a box to pick it. A new source goes above the selected one (on top if none is selected), hidden, with its settings open under its row so you can set it up straight away. Pages not in the scene yet are listed first, and Add all unused pages adds every one of them, hidden. Each row has the source's On / Off (a pill per output once there's more than one), an eye to preview it on its own and a pencil for its settings (name, address and page options, size, fade, position, its Stream Deck links, Move up, Move down and Remove); Done closes them. The list is top layer first: drag a source up or down to move it between layers.
  • Export sources saves the scene's sources as a .zip, with the images and videos they use (untick Export with their images and videos to leave them out), to keep as a backup or move to another Mac. Import sources replaces every source in the scene with the ones in an exported .zip (or its scene.json), after asking. Imported sources start hidden and keep their Stream Deck keys. Their images and videos go in the media folder; a file already there with the same name but different contents isn't overwritten, the imported one is kept as name (2) instead. Exported without media, the files have to be in the other Studio's media folder already; the Output page lists any that are missing.
  • Page options: a Studio graphic's link options listed on the Devices tab (position, heading, division, round, animate away and so on) show as fields under its Address, and are saved in the address.
  • On / Off fades a source in or out with its own Fade time in milliseconds (0 is a cut).
  • Position and scale place it on the frame; Full frame fits it to 1920 × 1080. Page size is the size a page is drawn at (1920 × 1080 for full-frame graphics).
  • Load the page fresh each time it's shown (on by default) reloads a page as it fades in so its animation replays, and unloads it when hidden. Untick it to keep a page running so it shows instantly. Refresh page reloads it now.
  • Animations that animate away switch themselves off: once a graphic has animated away and left the frame clear, its source is switched off in the scene (the Sources list and its Stream Deck key show it off), rather than staying on as an empty layer. That's The Clash and the winner animation with Animate away after set, a person lower third with Animate away after, positions with At the end: Animate away, the logo animation and the break ad with At the end: Animates away (their default), and the super shot and hot streak animations when they play once. Pages that stay on screen or wait for something to happen (super shots and hot streaks playing by themselves, the countdown, the scoreboard) stay on. This needs a scene window open (the Output page's Live View, its pop-out or a display or DeckLink output) to see the animation finish.
  • Preview opens the selected source on its own in a new window, the way the Preview buttons on the Devices tab do; the eye beside a source's On / Off does the same from the list. A preview opens at half size (960 x 540): the page is still laid out at 1080p, just scaled down, on a checkerboard so the see-through parts show, so a few fit on one screen. Resize the window to make it bigger.
  • Videos start from the beginning each time they're shown (untick to carry on), can loop, and fade out when they finish unless looping. A video that fades out when it finishes is switched off in the scene, so the Sources list and its Stream Deck key show it off and the next On plays it again. Sound turns a video's sound on or off, with a volume; it plays through the Mac's audio output, or in the SDI of a Blackmagic output. Restart video starts it again.
  • ProRes: the pages can't play ProRes, so a .mov added to the media folder is converted to a WebM beside it (keeping a ProRes 4444 clip's transparency; the Output page warns when a .mov has none to keep, such as H.264), and sources using it switch over when it's done. The original is kept. The Mac app includes ffmpeg to do this (running from a copy of the code instead, run vendor/ffmpeg/build.sh once or brew install ffmpeg). MP4 and WebM play as they are.
  • Media folder: the Output page's Media folder (at the bottom, folded until you open it) lists every song, video and image added (under Music, Add image or video, or on the Sponsors page), with its length, size and where it's used: sources, page themes (?theme=), sequence music (in any competition), playlists, the super shot song, the music player and sponsors' ads. Remove deletes a file for good (with a levelled song's original) after asking, and says where it's used first. A file on air (a source showing it, or the song playing) can't be removed until it's off. Playlists, the super shot song and sponsors' ads let go of it; sources, page themes and sequence music keep its name, shown as (missing), so their settings and Stream Deck keys stay until you pick another file. A .mov and the WebM converted from it are separate files.
  • Sequences: play the scene's sources one after another, for the run-up to a game, a quarter break or full time. + New sequence on the Output page opens a list of steps: each step turns on the sources you pick and stays for a set time (seconds), until it finishes (its videos end, or pages that switch themselves off when done; it moves on after the video's length plus 3 s, or a minute for a page, if the end never comes), until the game moves on (the clock starts, or the next break or full time) or until I press Next. As a step ends, sources the next step doesn't use go off with their own fades. A step can also be a scene (+ Add scene: a starting or reset point; what the scene turns on stays on under the next steps and after the sequence; it goes straight on or holds like any step) or another sequence (+ Add sequence: its steps play there, so a mini sequence is built once and used in many; a sequence can't go inside itself). A sequence can be all sources, all scenes, all sequences or a mix. Tick Hide to keep one off the list (a mini sequence used inside others); it still plays inside them, when its start comes and from the Stream Deck, and Show hidden brings the hidden ones back to the list. Starts is by hand, N minutes before the picked game's scheduled start (only if Studio was running at that moment: opened later, the sequence is left for you to play by hand), at the breaks you tick (quarter, half and three quarter time) or at full time, a few seconds after the siren (from the scoreboard's clock, the PlayHQ scorer's or yours; no extra PlayHQ calls). While it waits to start its card says Starting with Start now and Cancel. Automatic sequences on above the cards turns all of that off and on (remembered). Repeat plays it once, until stopped, until play resumes or until N minutes before the start (it finishes the step on screen, then ends), and Then goes to a scene, turns off what it turned on, or leaves everything as it is; a sequence that plays on more than one output has a Then for each. A sequence only turns off sources it turned on itself. While one plays, its card shows a dot per step, the step's time left and the whole sequence's Total (at least when part of it waits for Next, the game or a page; This time round when it repeats), with Next (end the step now), Pause and Stop (ends it and does its Then). Going to a scene, playing another sequence or hiding everything stops it without its Then; turning the step's source off by hand moves to the next step. One plays at a time, it keeps going with the Output page closed, and nothing is playing after Studio restarts. Sequences belong to the competition in use (saved in its folder as sequences.json) and change with Use this one; the sources and scenes are shared. Stream Deck: /deck/sequence/<name> plays one (/toggle stops it on a second press, /stop stops it), /deck/sequence/next, pause and stop act on whatever is playing (/deck/sequence/advance is Next only when the step on now is Until I press Next, and does nothing otherwise), /deck/sequence/now and cancel on one about to start, and /deck/sequence/auto/on, off or toggle set the switch. The break lengths (Competitions setup, Game rules) let the editor say how many times a break sequence fits in each break.
  • Run order: tonight's plan, above Scenes and Sequences: a numbered list of sequences and scenes for the picked output, in the order they play (Run-up, Walk out, Live, Quarter time, Live, Half time…). Add… puts a scene or sequence at the end; rows drag, or move with their arrows, and × takes one out. Play next plays the head now (a sequence starts, a scene is gone to) and Skip drops it without playing it; stepping through a sequence is the Sequences panel's Next. A sequence that ends looks at the run order first and plays what is queued after it; its own Then runs only when nothing is queued, so a sequence built for one night still works alone on another. Whatever is at the head leaves the list as it starts, however it was started. A game trigger puts its sequence at the head (with its few seconds and Start now / Cancel as usual) rather than starting it blind; Cancel takes it out again, and Next plays it when the ball is dead. Playing a scene or sequence by hand leaves the list alone unless it was the head, and nothing in the list starts by itself. The status board's Next line and the bar's chip say what Next will do. A sequence the run order starts plays on every output it plays on together when Play next is pressed; when the list moves on by itself, another of its outputs joins only if nothing is playing there and it didn't just play the sequence that ended (that one does its own Then). Its row says, for each other output, what follows it there. Saved… keeps tonight's order under a name (Save tonight's order as…), loads one in place of what is queued, deletes one or clears the list; the list and the saved orders live with the competition's sequences, so a relaunch picks the night up where it was. Stream Deck: /deck/runorder/next, skip, clear, add/<sequence or scene> and load/<saved order> (/deck/output/<output>/runorder/… for another output's list); the plugin's Run order key presses these, names what plays next in its colour and lights while something is queued. The existing /deck/sequence/next still ends the step on screen.

  • NDI® output (Mac app): sends the scene over the network as NDI sources, in the video format you pick (1080p at 23.98 to 120 fps, the same rates as a Blackmagic device, or 720p at 25 to 60 fps): fill and key as two sources (Source name Fill and Source name Key) for a switcher's fill and key inputs, chroma as one source over a solid colour (the same colour setting as the chroma display), or with transparency as one source with alpha for receivers that key on NDI's own transparency. They show up on the network as this Mac's name with the Source name in brackets (the output's name, for example Output 2 Fill, and it follows the output when it's renamed; tick Use my own name to type another; a source that changes name has to be picked again on the receiver). Each source only sends while something is receiving it, and the Output page says how many are. It needs NDI's runtime for Mac installed (free, the libNDI installer from ndi.link/NDIRedistV6Apple; Studio also uses the copy inside NDI Tools' apps if it finds one, as long as that copy is signed by NDI's makers, Vizrt or NewTek: a library runs inside Studio, so an unsigned file by that name is left alone and the log says so); the Output page says if it isn't, with where to get it, and finds it as soon as it's installed. Use a wired network: each 1080p source is roughly 100 to 150 Mbit/s at 50 or 60 fps and about twice that at 100 or 120 (fill and key is two of them), more than Wi-Fi carries steadily, so over Wi-Fi the receiver loses frames and moving graphics such as the ticker stutter. Pick the frame rate your switcher or stream runs at (1080p30 for a 30 fps stream): a higher one costs more of the network, and the receiver drops the extra frames anyway. Once a minute while something is receiving, the app log says how many frames a second went out and how many new pictures the scene drew; both at the full rate with a choppy picture means the network lost them. Sound plays through the Mac's audio output, not over NDI. Output on/off and the /deck/output links work the same. NDI® is a registered trademark of Vizrt NDI AB.

In 4K each NDI and Blackmagic output sends 2160p at the frame rate picked for it (2160p50 for 1080p50) and keeps everything else: its scenes, sources, NDI source names, Stream Deck keys and links, and its switcher key. Press HD and it's exactly as before. A Blackmagic device without that 2160p format (an HD UltraStudio), or a 720p NDI format, stays as it is and its card says so; displays are left alone, as a 4K screen set to look like 1920 × 1080 already draws at 4K. In 4K a Blackmagic output's video format list also has the device's own 4K formats (2160p50 and so on, as the device lists them) to pick directly; HD hides them again and sends the 1080p format at the same rate. Use 4K only with 4K gear: an HD switcher can't take 2160p. Any graphic's own link also draws sharp at 4K: in a browser source or window set to 3840 × 2160 it draws itself at twice the size, with nothing added to the link.

Each output is set up like the first (a chroma display, fill and key displays, the Blackmagic device or NDI) and has a name, a number and a colour: 1 orange, 2 pink, 3 blue, 4 green. Two outputs can't share a display, the Blackmagic device (one output) or an NDI source name: the earlier output keeps it and the Output page says so. Several outputs can each send NDI, each under its own source name (its own output name unless one is typed), and two chroma outputs can go to two displays over HDMI, each with its own key colour. Output 1 is the primary: the plain /deck/layer links act on it. Every output plays its own sound: the videos and animations on it, the music when Music plays on ticks it (Output 1 unless set; a sequence's music plays on every output playing the sequence), and the mics whose Goes to ticks it. Scenes and sequences are shared by every output, and each output plays its own: a click on a scene or sequence plays it on the output picked over the Scene View. Each output has its own current scene and plays one sequence at a time, so the same scene or sequence can be on two outputs at once; coloured dots on scene cards, and on sequence cards each output's tick in its colour with how it is there (Playing, Paused, Starting or Stopped (it ended there, played out or cut short); a hollow dot and "off" while that output isn't sending, as it still plays there for when it's turned on), show where. Hide all (under Sources) clears one output only and its label names it: the one the list shows when Show is set to one output, else the picked output. A sequence's Plays on ticks (on its card, in each output's colour, and in its editor) pick the outputs it plays on together: started by itself, or played on any of them (a click with that output's tab picked, or its Stream Deck key), it starts on all of them at once, and each output has its own Then for when it ends there (Output 1 back to the live scene, a camera's keyer output off, say). Played on an output it isn't ticked for, it plays there alone, and /deck/output/<output>/sequence/<name>/only plays it on that output alone even when it's ticked for more. Stop on a card, /deck/…/sequence/stop and Next act on one output; /toggle and /deck/…/sequence/<name>/stop stop it on every output it plays on, each doing its own Then. A sequence plays on its outputs whether or not they're sending, so turning one on mid-sequence shows it where it's up to; an output it wasn't started on doesn't join in until it's played again. An output it plays on that is removed is left out (stopping what played there); with all of them removed, the sequence doesn't start by itself (rather than going to air on Output 1) until another is ticked, and the log says so. A Sponsor ads step off Output 1 plays its ads in that output's own Sponsor ad sources (named after it, and removed with it). Only Output 1 and outputs with Counts for sponsors ticked (on the output's card, off to start) go in the sponsor report, as Studio can't tell whether a camera's keyer or a commentary monitor is on the stream; time on a ticked output is listed as "Ladder (Wide camera)", and a sponsor on two outputs at once counts once. A Sponsor ads step on an output that doesn't count shows whose turn it is without taking it, so the ads on air keep their order. /deck/scene/<name> and /deck/sequence/<name> play on Output 1; /deck/output/<output>/scene/<name>, next, prev and again, and /deck/output/<output>/sequence/<name> (with /play, /toggle, /stop or /only), next, advance, pause, stop, now and cancel play on that output. Sources are built once and shared; each output has its own on and off for every source, as coloured numbered buttons in the Sources list (a new output starts with everything off). Show above the list narrows it to one output, the tabs over the Scene View show each output, and Pop out opens the one picked. Stream Deck: /deck/output/<output>/on, off and toggle for one output (its name as a link, or its number), /deck/output/all/… for every one, and /deck/output/<output>/layer/<source>/on, off, toggle and /deck/output/<output>/layers/off for one output's sources (the Source settings list them). Each output with its own graphics runs its pages once more, so watch the Mac's load with three or four: while an output is on, its card shows its CPU (a percentage of one processor core), memory and the frame rate its pages draw at against what they should (in amber when it falls under 90%), with Studio's total and the graphics processor under the status, and the log has a Performance line once a minute.

With Chroma (a display, or NDI's chroma signal) picked, Chroma key safe is ticked unless you untick it, and the Output page warns that the sources are switched to it: every graphic and animation goes out with solid panels, no soft shadows, glows, light rays or flashes (hard shadows stay, made solid), and nothing half see-through, so fades become cuts (sources cut instead of fading) and soft edges are hard. Options that only fade, like the countdown's fade and the ad's glow, are skipped, and the source's options say so. Titles, logos, light and dark modes and every other setting work as usual. The Output page also lists any competition or club colour close to the key colour (a keyer would cut holes in it); logos can't be checked, so look at those. For a graphic used on its own at its link and keyed on a colour, add ?chromaKey=true to its link.

Set up, then Look for switchers, finds every ATEM on the network the way ATEM Software Control does (the first time, macOS asks to let Studio use the local network: say Allow); pick one and Studio connects and remembers it by name, so it finds it again if the venue's router gives it another address. Or type its address (as set in ATEM Setup; an ATEM ships as 192.168.10.240) works too. The card shows the switcher's inputs by the names set in ATEM Software Control, red on program and green on preview: click one to put it on preview, then Cut or Auto (the switcher's own transition) to take it. Fade to black, and each upstream and downstream key on and off (red while on air; a downstream key's Auto takes it at its own rate), are there too. Studio reads what the switcher has and offers only that:
    • ATEM Mini and Mini Pro: 4 inputs, one upstream key, one downstream key. Fill and key into the downstream key takes two inputs and leaves two cameras; a chroma key needs the upstream key (one input, three cameras left).
    • ATEM Mini Extreme: 8 inputs, four upstream keys, two downstream keys.
    A link or button for something the switcher doesn't have (a second downstream key on a Mini Pro, say) is refused with what it does have.
    Into the switcher as: each output's setup (under Video output) says which of the switcher's keys its graphics come into and which inputs its fill and key are plugged into (a chroma output needs just one). The usual key is downstream key 1 for Output 1 and upstream keys 2 to 4 for Outputs 2 to 4; a chroma output goes on an upstream key, as only those key on a colour. Pick another key, or Not keyed by the switcher. Studio then checks that key's settings in the switcher, read only, and lists what doesn't suit the output: its fill and key sources, its type (Chroma for a chroma output, Luma for fill and key) and Pre Multiplied Key (on), and asks for a Blackmagic device's Straight to be off. It also says when the output can't be keyed as it is: a chroma output on a downstream key, NDI with transparency or fill only (an ATEM needs a key signal or a colour), two outputs on the same key, or a key the switcher hasn't got. For a chroma key, pick the key colour in ATEM Software Control with its sample picker. Nothing is sent to the switcher until it's pressed.
    Cameras per output: an output on an upstream key can say which cameras its graphics go with (ticked under Into the switcher as; the outputs' own fill and key inputs aren't offered). Studio's cuts, autos and stingers, from the Switcher card, a Stream Deck key or a sequence or scene, then take that key on with those cameras and off with the others in the same transition, so the camera and its graphics change on the same frame (Studio picks the keys in the switcher's next transition and puts the BKGD and KEY buttons back as they were afterwards). A straight-to-program press brings the key straight after. With Keys follow the camera on (the Switcher card, on unless turned off), a cut made on the switcher itself or in ATEM Software Control brings the keys a frame or two later. It only acts on a change of camera, so a key turned on or off by hand stays as it is, and it sends nothing on connecting. An output with no cameras ticked, on a downstream key (over every camera), or whose key check shows a problem is left alone. The output's card says On air with Bench or Waiting for Bench, and the Switcher card underlines each key in its output's colour. The switcher's own buttons, ATEM Software Control and other controllers keep working alongside Studio, and the card follows what they do. Studio sends nothing to the switcher by itself: not when it connects, and not when it reconnects. If the switcher stops answering (a pulled cable, a power cut), or is off when Studio connects, the card turns red within about five seconds and Studio keeps trying; anything pressed meanwhile is refused, not saved up for later, and the log says so. Studio connects when it goes on air and lets go when it goes off air. Stream Deck: /deck/switcher/cut, auto and ftb; /deck/switcher/preview/<input> and program/<input> (its number, or its name such as Camera%202); /deck/switcher/usk/<n>/on, off or toggle; /deck/switcher/dsk/<n>/on, off, toggle or auto; and /deck/switcher/macro/<n> (a macro saved on the switcher, numbered from 1 as in ATEM Software Control). With no stinger in the scene, a stinger is an auto; if the page doesn't start within 3 s, it's a plain cut, and the log says which. A second stinger while one plays is left out (a press, a Stream Deck key, or a sequence or scene's own Stinger to), so a double tap never makes two cuts. Stinger to a camera that's already on program plays the stinger with no cut (for a change of graphics under it). Actions at the same moment go in order, so a preview then a cut works. A step's actions still to come are called off when the step ends or the sequence stops; a scene's when another scene is picked. A scene step does the scene's actions too. The editor offers the inputs and keys the connected switcher has; one it hasn't got (upstream key 2 on a Mini Pro, say) is shown in red and skipped, with a line in the log. If the switcher isn't answering when an action's moment comes, it's skipped and logged, never done late. Scenes and sequences with switcher actions show 🎥 by their name.

The scene only changes when you change it, so it adds no PlayHQ requests beyond those of the pages in it.

Scoreboard control in detail

Every graphic uses the game night, game and court set on the Scoreboard control. That page is laid out for the operator: the game on the board in one bar with Follow PlayHQ or Manual and the next game the board will switch to, then the two scores and the clock as the biggest things on the page, with their keys printed on the buttons (A/Z and K/M, Shift for a super shot, Space for the clock); the graphics, team lists, finals and the rest are tabs beneath, with a preview of the board beside them, and the game's own set-up (night, court, automatic selection, how often PlayHQ is read) is the Game section at the bottom, which Change game… opens and which folds to its title. A setting added to a page's own link (like ?division=2 or ?round=6) overrides it for that page only.

  • Game night: the games list has every division's games on one date, whatever round each division is up to, so one division on Round 10 and another on Finals Round 1 the same night are listed together, each game with its own round. Automatic picks the first game night in the next 5 days, counting today, so a Tuesday night is picked from the Friday before (the number of days is on the competition setup page). Otherwise it's the last night played. Each night in the list names its rounds. Pick a night to use it, then set it back to Automatic. Pages that show one round (like the fixture) use each division's round on that night. On a day with several rounds, it moves on to each round as its first game comes up.
  • Always a game on: when Studio starts, or the scoreboard is cleared, it puts a game on straight away with Follow PlayHQ on: the game for now on the court, otherwise this game night's first game on that court, otherwise its first game. The board is only left empty if PlayHQ can't be reached or the competition isn't found (check the PlayHQ link and competition on the competition setup page).
  • Game: Pick the game automatically for court is ticked at the start of every game night, on the night's court (e.g. Indoor 01 or just 1). It shows the first game of the day on that court, then switches to each next game when its electronic scoring starts (10 minutes before by default, set on the setup page), with the quarter back to Q1 and a full clock. A final (a round with "Final" in its name) stays on until PlayHQ marks it finished, in case of extra time. With a game night picked by hand, it follows that night's games on the court by their time: the game due now, otherwise the next one to come, otherwise (a night already played) its last game.
  • Production Planner: the Production Planner plans the court Studio streams each game night and who is on the mic for each game. When it has a court for tonight (the game night picked by hand, otherwise today), that court is set here when the night arrives, which also ticks automatic selection, and again if the plan's court changes during the night. A night with no plan (the night before's plan counts, so mostly before the Planner is first used) uses the competition's Fallback court to follow. Picking another game night, or another competition, is a new night. The Commentators graphic and Commentator stats show whoever is planned for the game on the scoreboard, and a night that hasn't been planned takes the plan of the night before it. Beside the court it says where it came from: from the Production Planner, fallback from Competitions setup or changed by hand for tonight. A court changed or a game picked by hand stays for the rest of that night, restarts included, and the Planner flags it.
  • Overrides: picking a game from the list turns automatic selection off until you tick it again, change the court, or the next game night starts. Pressing a score button switches scores to manual, and any clock button switches the clock to manual.
  • Follow PlayHQ: the score and quarter come from PlayHQ's live scoring every 5 seconds, and Follow the PlayHQ scorer's clock runs the clock from the scorer's clock too. Switching scores between Follow PlayHQ and Manual (on Scoreboard control, the Stream Deck, by pressing a score button or by picking a game) turns the clock option on or off to match, and Scoreboard control says so. Changing the clock after that still wins: untick the box or press a clock button to run the clock by hand while scores follow PlayHQ.
  • Pages that use the picked game's division: ladder and finals (without ?division=), ladder bar, top shooters, title, match up, The Clash, winner celebration, team lists, shooting stats and full time. The fixture, results, ticker and up next use the round.
  • Stream Deck: /deck/auto/on, /deck/auto/off, /deck/auto/toggle, /deck/court/2, /deck/clock/playhq/on and /deck/ticker/faster / slower. The Devices tab has the rest under Links, with Copy buttons, and the plugin. A score key switches scoring to Manual (Follow PlayHQ stops for the score and the clock), and a clock key stops the clock following the PlayHQ scorer.

Socials in detail

The Socials tab makes pictures of a game night for Instagram and other social media, from the fixture, results and ladders Studio already has. Pick the game night (by date, so every division playing that night is in it), then Upcoming (before the night) or Results (after it; a night with results opens on Results) and the sizes: Square 1080 x 1080, Portrait 1080 x 1350 and Story 1080 x 1920. Upcoming pictures:

  • Game night fixture: every game of the night on every court, by time, with the logos, division and court.
  • Match up: one per game, the two logos big with the date, time, court and venue.
  • On the stream: the court Studio streams that night, each game's division and who is calling it, from the Production Planner (plan the night first).

Recordings in detail

For each PlayHQ game on the scoreboard while on air, Studio keeps a recording of what PlayHQ said and what went to air, timed: PlayHQ's answers (live score, clock, quarter, line ups, fixtures, ladders), each scoreboard press and Stream Deck press, and every change to the scene, the board and the answers the graphics read. There's no video or sound in it, only what the graphics need, a megabyte or so a game. Each recording follows a PlayHQ game: it starts when the game is due on the scoreboard (as many minutes before its scheduled start as automatic game selection on the competition setup page says, 10 by default) and stops a set time after PlayHQ says it's finished (Keep recording for … minutes after full time, 10 by default, 0 to 60), when the scoreboard moves on to another game, or when Studio goes off air. Being on air with no PlayHQ game due records nothing, and a game PlayHQ already says is finished isn't recorded. Each recording starts with the last of everything Studio already knew (the fixtures and ladders PlayHQ gave, the scene, the board), so it stands on its own. The Recordings tab lists each game's recording (named after when it started and the teams), its length and size, with Download (the .jsonl.gz file, to keep or send in as test data) and Remove, and says when the next one starts. Record each PlayHQ game on air is on unless you turn it off; turning it off stops the recording being made. The Output page's status board (Show) has the same switch as Record and Stop, so a recording can be stopped or started without leaving it. A recording older than 60 days is removed when Studio starts, and so is an old one with no game in it. Recordings stay on this Mac (in Recordings/ of the data folder), never in a backup or iCloud, and hold nothing secret: no keys, devices, cookies or private answers. A recording also replays a game's graphics at 4K (below).

4K replay: Replay beside a game puts that game's graphics back on every output exactly as they went to air: the scene, the scoreboard and what each graphic read, from the recording. Turn on 4K on the Output page and the outputs send it at 2160p, sharp, to key over the 4K camera recording of the game in a 4K switcher or recorder; Studio doesn't play the video. It starts paused at the game. To sync, pause the video on a moment you can see (the first centre pass), pick the same moment in the list of what went on air (Scoreboard on, Q1…) and Jump there, then press Play on both together; Nudge moves the graphics a frame (40 ms) or a second either way. While it runs the live scene and scoreboard are kept as they were and can't be changed (the outputs can), nothing counts for the sponsors and the replay isn't recorded, and a red strip on every Studio page says so, with Stop. Stop puts the live graphics back.

Following a HyperDeck: with the 4K video on a HyperDeck, set Follow to A HyperDeck's timecode and type its IP address (on its setup page). The replay then goes where the HyperDeck's timecode says, as a time of day on the game's night: play, stop and scrub the HyperDeck and the graphics go with it, so they never drift, and Studio's own Play is the HyperDeck's. Studio only reads its timecode over the network (its control protocol, port 9993) and never plays, stops or records anything on it. If the HyperDeck's clock and this Mac's differed on the night, sync once: stop the HyperDeck on a moment you can see, pick the same moment in the list and press Jump there. Nudge then moves the graphics against the video. The sync is kept for each recording. The recording needs time-of-day timecode (the HyperDeck's timecode set to Time of day, or timecode from the switcher).

Following the SDI input: any player can drive the replay through the UltraStudio instead. Send its SDI into the UltraStudio's SDI input (a HyperDeck's monitor or SDI output is enough; the 4K picture can go to the switcher as usual) and set Follow to Timecode into the UltraStudio (SDI). Studio reads the timecode that comes with each frame (only the timecode, not the picture) and follows it the same way: playing, stopped and scrubbing, with Jump there and Nudge to sync. HDMI carries no timecode, so the HDMI input can't be used. If the UltraStudio can't read its input while it sends the graphics, Studio says so beside Follow. Only in the Mac app.

Stream Deck: the plugin's Replay key plays or pauses, jumps to the next or previous moment, nudges or stops the 4K replay or practice game, whichever is running, and lights while one runs; or the links /deck/replay/toggle, next, prev, nudge/-40, stop and /deck/practice/toggle, next, prev, stop (the Devices tab lists them all under Links).

Practice games: Practise beside a game on the Recordings tab plays it back as if it were live. Studio asks PlayHQ nothing; the recording answers instead, with the game's date and times moved to now, so the game starts a few minutes from now: as many as A practice game starts in says (5 to begin with, 0 to 60). Everything runs as on a real night: the scoreboard follows the score, clock and quarter, sequences and game triggers fire, and the outputs can be on. Jump to Start, each quarter or Full time, Pause (the clock stands still) and Stop are at the top of the Recordings tab, and a red strip on every Studio page says a practice game is running, with Stop. Nothing it does counts for the sponsors' reports, it isn't recorded as a game, and Stop puts the scoreboard back as it was. Use it to rehearse a night, try a new sequence or show Studio to someone.

Demo games: Demo beside a recorded game plays it as one of the competition in use's games, for showing Studio to a league in their own colours. Pick a division and one of its finished games: Studio takes that game's two teams (with their logos and colours) and athletes, and the division's other teams, and everything else (the score, clock, quarters, moments and timing) comes from the recording. Athletes are matched by position, so the recorded GS's goals are their GS's; anyone with no match is left unnamed and their goals go to the team. A game with no line up (or a short one) takes the team's season squad, by the position each played most. While the demo runs, its Athletes table shows who each recorded athlete is shown as, and any of them can be changed. Save demo keeps it by name (teams, athletes, Swap) under Saved demos, to start again in one click; saved demos stay on this Mac. Swap home and away puts the teams the other way round, so either can be the winner. The competition's setup comes from PlayHQ, so a demo needs the internet. Studio never plays video: play the recorded game wherever you like and lay Studio's output over it. A red strip says a demo game is running, as for practice.

Scene sequences

A sequence plays sources one after another on the Output page, so the run-up to a game, each quarter break and the post-game wrap can run themselves. You build it as a short list of steps. Each step says what comes on and how long it stays: a set time for a still graphic, or until it finishes for a video or an animation. A music track can play under a sequence, or across several sequences grouped as a set.

Nothing here is built yet. Your answers to the questions at the end decide the details, then it ships in three small PRs.

Where it fits

The Output page already has two layers of control. Sequences add a third on top and reuse both, so nothing you have set up changes.

SourcesPages, images and videos drawn as layers, each with its own fade. You turn them on and off by hand or from the Stream Deck.
ScenesSaved looks: which sources are on and off. Going to a scene switches sources to match.
SequencesSources or scenes played in order over time, started by you, the Stream Deck, or the game itself.

Run-up and Walk out are two sequences on purpose. The game clock decides when each starts, and each sequence only has to say what plays in order. That keeps every sequence a plain list rather than a timeline editor.

A step

A step is one or more sources that come on together, and a length. When the step ends, its sources go off with their own fades as the next step's sources fade on, so steps crossfade using the fade times you already set per source.

For a set time Pages, images

Shown for N seconds. The default for anything that doesn't end on its own. New steps start at 20 s.

Until it finishes Videos, animations

A video plays its length, which the editor shows (0:10). An animation set to animate away (The Clash with an out time, Winner, Logo, Lower third) ends when it switches itself off. A safety limit moves on if the end never arrives.

Until the game moves on Holds

Waits for the next thing on court: the clock starting, play resuming after a break, or full time. Usually the last step, so a delayed start or a long break just holds.

Until I press Next Manual

Holds until you press Next on the Output page or the Stream Deck. Handy for a presenter piece or a sponsor read of unknown length.

Two more things a sequence can say:

  • Keep on for the whole sequence. Sources that stay on from the first step to the last, like the countdown and title during the run-up. They go off when the sequence ends, unless the ending scene keeps them on.
  • Go to a scene. A step can be a scene instead of sources. It is almost always the last step, and saying "then go to Game" is how a sequence hands back to the normal game look.

Starting and ending

By hand

Press Play on the sequence's card, or the Stream Deck key. Every sequence can be started by hand, including the automatic ones.

Before the start

N minutes before the scheduled start of the picked game. Uses the same start time as the countdown and auto-select, so it follows whichever game is picked.

At a break or full time

Quarter time, half time, three quarter time (tick which), or full time, each with a short delay. Comes from the clock Studio already follows: the PlayHQ scorer's clock when following, or your manual clock.

Between a trigger and the start there is a short wait, such as 5 s after the siren. During that wait the card shows Starts in 0:05 with Delay +10 s, Start now and Cancel. A shot after the siren is the common case: press Delay on the Stream Deck once or twice, and the Full time sequence starts once the ball is dead and the score is final. Winner reads the score when it starts, so it shows the right team.

Automatic starts sit behind one switch on the Output page, Automatic sequences on. With it off, nothing starts by itself and every sequence still plays by hand. Each sequence picks how it ends:

  • Play once, Repeat until stopped, or Repeat until a game moment (2 minutes before the start, play resumes).
  • Then go to a scene, turn off what the sequence turned on, or leave everything as it is.

Mockup: the Output page

Sequences get their own strip under Scenes, with the same card style. A playing card shows a dot per step, the step on now and the time left. The switch for automatic starts sits at the top of the strip, so you see at a glance whether the game will drive the screen.

Music and sequence sets

A sequence can have a music track. The music can start with the sequence, with or without a fade in. It can stop when the sequence ends, with or without a fade out, or carry on across several sequences grouped as a set.

Where the music comes from

Audio files Works

Add an MP3, M4A (AAC), WAV or AIFF to the media folder, the same way you add videos. These play anywhere Studio's video sound plays.

Your Music library Partly

Songs you bought from the iTunes Store or imported yourself are ordinary files, so Choose from Music can open the picker in the Music app's media folder and copy one in. Apple Music songs, streamed or downloaded, are copy-protected and only play inside Apple's own apps, so Studio can't play them. Apple Music's licence is also personal use only, and YouTube and Facebook mute or flag streams that use it.

Use music you're licensed to broadcast, such as royalty-free or production music, or tracks the league has rights to. That rule comes from the platforms, not from Studio.

Music on a sequence

Music is a new source type, Audio, in the source list. It has no picture, but it gets everything a video's sound gets: volume, fade, a Stream Deck on and off key, and the same sound path. Video sound plays through the Mac's output by default, or embedded in the Blackmagic signal if you turn that on. The Scene View sound rule still applies, so you never hear two copies. "Music starts" can also be "at step N", so the music can kick in on The Clash rather than at the top. With several tracks, they play in order and crossfade. If they all run out before the sequence ends, they start again.

Sequence sets

A set is a named chain of sequences that share one music track: Pre-game could be Run-up, then Walk out. The set starts when its first sequence starts, whether by hand or by trigger. Each next sequence starts when the one before it ends, or earlier if its own trigger arrives first. The music runs across the whole set and ends with the last sequence, using that sequence's "Music ends" setting.

Things to think about with music

  • Lowering it under other sound. The break ad or The Clash may have its own sound. Without ducking you'd get two tracks fighting, so the music dips while they play and comes back after.
  • Ending on time. A cut at first centre sounds like a mistake. "Fades to finish as the game starts" times the fade to land on the clock starting, and waits if the start is late.
  • Tracks of different loudness. Every track has its own volume, and Studio could even out loudness when a file is added (ffmpeg can measure and adjust it), so tracks from different sources sit at the same level.
  • Skipping an intro. Each track can start at a set point, such as 0:12, to skip a slow intro.
  • Quick control on the night. There's a Fade music out button on the playing card and the Stream Deck, plus a music mute key. Stop on a sequence or set fades the music rather than cutting it.
  • Your commentary. If commentators talk over the run-up, the music level is usually set in the switcher or mixer. Studio's volume is for balancing tracks against each other.

While it plays

Your rule is that manual controls are never taken away, so the sequence works around you rather than locking anything.

If you already pressed Stop on a break sequence, it won't restart for that same break. If the game clock jumps (a correction from the scorer), Studio waits for the clock to settle for a few seconds before it treats a break or full time as real.

Stream Deck

Each sequence gets links in the same style as scenes, listed on the Stream Deck page:

The FirstPass Studio plugin gets a Sequence key: the name, lit while playing, with the step and time left on the key (for example "3/5 · 0:04"). Pressing it plays or stops.

How it works underneath

  • Sequences are saved in scene.json next to sources and scenes, so Export and Import sources carry them.
  • The sequence runs in Studio itself (in scene.js), not in the Output page. It keeps time with the Output page closed, and every output and the pop-out preview see the same thing.
  • "Until it finishes" listens for the same end signal that switches finished videos and animations off today. Video lengths come from the bundled ffmpeg when a file is added. The safety limit is the video length plus 3 s, or 60 s for a page.
  • Game moments come from the scoreboard state Studio already keeps: scheduled start, clock running, quarter and the PlayHQ break flag, and the final siren. No extra PlayHQ calls.
  • Every start, step, skip and stop is written to the debug log with its reason ("Started Quarter break: half time").
  • If Studio restarts mid-sequence, it does not pick up where it was. The screen stays as it was left and the next trigger starts fresh.

How it ships

1. Sequences by handThe Sequences strip, the editor, Play, Next, Pause, Stop, all four step lengths except "until the game moves on", and Then.
2. Stream DeckThe /deck/sequence/… links, the plugin's Sequence key, and the Stream Deck page and home page docs.
3. Game triggersBefore the start, breaks and full time, "until the game moves on", "repeat until", and the Automatic sequences switch.
4. Music and setsAudio sources and files, the Music rows, ducking, sequence sets, and the music Stream Deck links. "Fades to finish as the game starts" needs PR 3.

Game triggers come last, as planned in the OBS replacement design. The first two PRs are useful on their own: you can fire a quarter-break sequence from one Stream Deck key.

Questions for you

Each has the answer I'd go with, so "yes to all" works.

  1. Should steps be sources, scenes, or both?I'd go with sources for each step, plus a scene as the "Then". Scenes as steps would also turn off things you put on by hand.
  2. How long are Thunder League breaks?I've guessed about 3 minutes at quarter time and three quarter time, and 5 at half time. That's only used for the "plays about 2½ times" hint, since "repeat until play resumes" handles the real length.
  3. Should the game start sequences by itself, and should the switch remember its setting?I'd go with yes, behind the one Automatic sequences switch, remembered between nights. You could also have it start off each time Studio opens, if you'd rather opt in each night.
  4. When you go to a scene by hand while a sequence plays, should the sequence stop?I'd go with yes. The other way, the sequence keeps going over your scene, felt more surprising to me.
  5. Is "repeat until 2 minutes before, then a separate Walk out sequence" the right way to build the run-up?I'd go with this rather than one sequence with timed sections. It keeps the editor a plain list.
  6. Does one sequence at a time work for you?I'd go with one at a time. Two at once (a sponsor loop over a break loop) would need rules for who wins a source.
  7. Should finals and carnival days use different sequences?I'd keep sequences shared by every competition, like sources and scenes, and leave per-competition sequences for later if you need them.
  8. Is "Choose from Music" for your own purchased or imported files OK in place of Apple Music?I'd go with this. Apple Music songs can't be played outside Apple's apps, and they aren't licensed for streams.
  9. Where does Studio's sound reach the stream today: the Mac's output into the ATEM, or embedded in the UltraStudio's SDI?This decides which output the music uses by default. I'd keep the default the same as video sound.
  10. Should music dip under a video's own sound automatically?I'd go with yes, to 25 %, changeable per sequence.
  11. Should Studio even out loudness when you add a track?I'd go with yes, as a tick box that's on by default, keeping your original file.
